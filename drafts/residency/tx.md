# Texas — residency agreement and involuntary discharge

- **State:** Texas (TX)
- **Retrieval date:** 2026-07-30
- **Researcher:** agent (RightsBook residency research pass)
- **Primary facility category researched:** HCS Program provider-owned or controlled residential
  settings — **three-person residence**, **four-person residence**, and **host home/companion
  care** (when the individual does not own the residence or lease it from another person).
  26 TAC Chapter 263, Subchapter F.
- **Categories NOT covered by the findings below (see Q1 note and Verification notes):**
  ICF/IID (26 TAC Ch. 551), assisted living (26 TAC Ch. 553 / H&S Code Ch. 247), Texas Home
  Living (TxHmL, 26 TAC Ch. 262 — non-residential), DBMD waiver (not researched), and HCS
  Support Family Services / Community Family Services.

**Headline:** Texas answers 42 CFR 441.301(c)(4)(vi)(A) by the opposite route from Minnesota.
It does not build a parallel administrative eviction appeal. It **forces the residential
agreement to be a lease and puts the parties squarely under Texas landlord/tenant law**, and
then layers on a 60-day cure period and a mandatory pre-eviction meeting that ordinary Texas
tenants do not get. The appeal against an eviction is therefore **judicial** (justice court).
A separate and genuinely **administrative** appeal (HHSC fair hearing, with a stay) exists on
the other track — termination of HCS *services* — and Texas ties the two together by allowing
eviction only for nonpayment or for termination of services.

---

## 1. Is a written residency/admission agreement required? Which regulation?

**Yes. Direct-fetch.** 26 TAC § 263.503(b) requires a **written residential agreement** for an
individual living in a three-person residence or four-person residence (with the program
provider), and for an individual in host home/companion care (with the host home/companion care
service provider) **if the individual does not own or lease the residence from another person**.

§ 263.503(c) prescribes 22 mandatory contents. § 263.503(d) adds process: at least **three
calendar days** to review, request changes, and sign; the agreement must be **fully executed
before** the individual begins living there (emergency exception: executed within **seven
calendar days**, with the emergency documented); and a copy to the individual/LAR within **three
business days** of full execution.

Companion setting rules: 26 TAC § 263.501 (Requirements for Home and Community-Based Settings)
and § 263.502 (Requirements for Program Provider Owned or Controlled Residential Settings —
bedroom privacy, choice of roommate, operable **lock on the bedroom door**, furnish/decorate,
control of schedule, access to food at any time, visitors at any time, physical accessibility).

*ICF/IID note:* no equivalent Texas residency-agreement rule was found. 26 TAC § 551.42(c)
adopts the federal ICF/IID conditions (42 CFR Part 483, Subpart I, §§ 483.400–483.480) as the
licensing standards. **Do not import the HCS answer into an ICF/IID question.**

## 2. Does state landlord/tenant law apply, or is there a carve-out?

**It applies, affirmatively and by rule. There is no carve-out. Direct-fetch.** This is the
strongest finding in Texas and the direct answer to the (vi)(A) hinge.

26 TAC § 263.503(c)(7)(A) requires the agreement to contain a provision that the parties
**agree the residential agreement is a "lease" as defined in Texas Property Code Chapter 92**
and that they are **subject to state law governing residential tenancies**, including Texas
Property Code **Chapters 24, 91, and 92** and **Texas Rule of Civil Procedure 510**.

§ 263.503(c)(8) then bars waiver: the agreement must state that the individual/LAR **is not
waiving any right or remedy provided to tenants under state law**, including the Texas Fair
Housing Act (Property Code Ch. 301), **and is not agreeing to any notice period shorter than
the notice period to which tenants are entitled under state law**.

One drafting wrinkle worth flagging to an advocate: § 263.503(c)(7)(B) says that in a conflict
between the agreement and state statutory law (including Property Code Chs. 91 and 92), **"to
the extent allowed by law … the provision in the residential agreement governs."** Read with
(c)(8), the agreement cannot use that clause to cut below tenant protections, but the clause is
there and is worth reading in any actual agreement.

## 3. Notice period(s) before an involuntary discharge, and short-notice exceptions

Texas splits this into two different actions. Keep them apart.

### 3a. Eviction from the residence (tenancy action)

Grounds are closed. § 263.503(c)(17) allows eviction **only** if (A) the individual/LAR fails to
pay room or board (late fees excluded), **or** (B) the individual's HCS Program services are
terminated. There is no "behavior," "needs exceed our capacity," or "at-will" ground in the rule.

Sequence for nonpayment (§ 263.503(h)–(j)):

1. Provider must **notify the service coordinator** of the delinquency and of the intent to
   evict. § 263.503(h)(1).
2. Provider must then **meet** with the individual/LAR — including the SSA representative payee
   if one exists — and the service coordinator, to discuss the alleged non-payment and options
   to prevent eviction. § 263.503(h)(2).
3. If still proceeding, provider gives a **written notice of proposed eviction allowing at least
   60 calendar days to pay** the delinquent room or board, with a copy to the service
   coordinator. § 263.503(c)(18), (h)(3).
4. If the money is paid inside that window, the provider **must not** give a notice to vacate or
   otherwise proceed. § 263.503(c)(19), (i).
5. Only if unpaid: the provider must report the non-payment (SSA, the probate court that
   appointed the guardian, or DFPS as an allegation of the LAR's exploitation or neglect), must
   meet again with the individual/LAR and service coordinator to discuss alternative living
   settings, and may then give a **written notice to vacate "in accordance with the residential
   agreement and state law"**, copying the service coordinator within one business day.
   § 263.503(j).
6. The state-law floor for that notice to vacate is **at least three days' written notice**
   under Tex. Prop. Code § 24.005(a) unless the lease contracts for a longer period — and
   § 263.503(c)(8) forbids the agreement from going shorter than state law.

**Short-notice exception: none found.** No rule was located letting a provider shorten the
60-day cure period or the statutory notice to vacate, and **no state agency pre-approval of an
eviction is required** (contrast California's 3-day/CCL-approval route). The service coordinator
must be notified and must be at the meeting, but the rule does not give the coordinator or HHSC
a veto. *Absence finding — see Verification notes.*

The only fast-exit provision found runs the other way: § 263.503(c)(13) requires a provision
letting **the individual** terminate the agreement before its expiration date without further
obligation if they permanently move out for any reason, including transferring providers, and
(c)(14) requires a **pro-rata refund** of room and board for days after a permanent move.

### 3b. Termination of HCS Program services (benefit action)

Termination is an **HHSC** action, not a provider action. A service coordinator *requests* it on
an HHSC Request for Termination of Services form; HHSC sends the notice. 26 TAC §§ 263.707(b)–(c),
263.708(b)–(c).

- **With advance notice** (§ 263.707): only for failure to meet the eligibility criteria in
  § 263.101(a)(1)–(4), (6), (c), (d). The service coordinator must first convene a service
  planning team meeting to try to resolve the situation.
- **Without advance notice** (§ 263.708): five closed grounds — institutional stay over 270
  consecutive days without an approved extension, confirmed death, a clear signed written
  statement that the individual no longer wants services, whereabouts unknown with mail returned
  and no forwarding address, or acceptance for Medicaid in another state.
- Timing of the advance notice is set by the fair-hearing rules rather than by Chapter 263: in
  Medicaid cases the written notice **"be mailed at least 10 days before the date the
  individual's Medicaid eligibility or service is scheduled to be terminated, suspended, or
  reduced."** 1 TAC § 357.11(b)(1)(B).

## 4. Is there an appeal, and is it ADMINISTRATIVE or JUDICIAL?

**Both exist, on different tracks. Say which one every time.**

- **Eviction → JUDICIAL.** The forum is Texas justice court under a forcible detainer suit,
  Property Code Chapter 24 and TRCP 510, which 26 TAC § 263.503(c)(7)(A) expressly makes
  applicable. The person defends in court, after the fact, on their own initiative. **No
  administrative appeal of an eviction was found.**
- **Termination / denial / suspension / reduction of HCS or CFC services → ADMINISTRATIVE.** An
  HHSC fair hearing under 26 TAC § 263.801, which routes to **1 TAC Chapter 357, Subchapter A
  (Uniform Fair Hearing Rules)**. See also § 263.704 (denial) and § 263.703 (denial of
  enrollment), which carry the same fair-hearing pointer.
- **Internal provider grievance:** not the mechanism here, and not to be conflated with either
  of the above. 26 TAC § 565.25 requires an HCS program provider to assess individual
  satisfaction at least annually and act within 60 days on areas of dissatisfaction — a quality
  process, **not** an appeal, with no adjudicator and no remedy.

Practical consequence of the closed eviction grounds in § 263.503(c)(17): if the eviction is
predicated on services being terminated, the person **can** reach an administrative forum — by
appealing the service termination — and, under § 263.707(d), the services continue while that
appeal is pending, which removes the predicate for the eviction. If the eviction is for
nonpayment of room and board, there is no administrative forum at all; it is justice court.

## 5. Must the NOTICE ITSELF state the appeal right?

**Split answer. Read carefully — this is the trap.**

- **The provider's eviction notices: NO.** Nothing in 26 TAC § 263.503 requires the written
  **notice of proposed eviction** (§ 263.503(c)(18), (h)(3)(A)) or the written **notice to
  vacate** (§ 263.503(j)(3)(A)) to state any appeal, hearing, or defense right. The 22 required
  contents of the residential agreement in § 263.503(c) likewise do not include an appeal
  process. **No such requirement found.** Places looked: 26 TAC §§ 263.501, 263.502, 263.503,
  263.701, 263.707, 263.708, 263.801; 1 TAC § 357.11.
- **HHSC's service-termination notice: YES.** 26 TAC § 263.707(c) and § 263.708(c) both provide
  that **"The notice includes the individual's right to request a fair hearing in accordance
  with § 263.801 of this chapter."** § 263.704(c) carries the same requirement for denials.
  Generally, 1 TAC § 357.11(a)(2)–(4) requires the agency to give timely and adequate notice of
  the right to a fair hearing, **"explain the right of appeal"** and **"explain the procedures
  for requesting an appeal"**, and (a)(6) to **"provide information about legal services
  available in the community."** For Medicaid, 1 TAC § 357.11(b)(1)(A) requires the notice to
  **"contain an explanation of the circumstances under which Medicaid is continued if a hearing
  is requested."**

So Texas does what Minnesota does — but only on the **services** notice, which comes from HHSC,
and not on the **housing** notice, which comes from the provider. The document that actually
tells a person to leave their home is not required to say anything about their rights.

## 6. Is there a stay / "services continue pending appeal"?

**Yes on the services track. No administrative stay on the eviction track.**

- **Services:** 26 TAC § 263.707(d) — if the individual/LAR requests a fair hearing **before the
  effective date of termination** stated in the notice, **"the program provider must provide
  services to the individual in the amounts authorized in the IPC while the appeal is pending."**
  Reinforced generally by 1 TAC § 357.11(b)(2), which bars the agency from acting before the
  hearing decision if the hearing was requested before the scheduled action date, subject to the
  federal exception where the sole issue is one of federal or state law or policy (in which case
  the agency must promptly notify the recipient in writing that services will be terminated,
  suspended, or reduced pending the decision).
- **No stay is attached to § 263.708** (termination without advance notice) — by construction,
  there is no pre-effective-date window.
- **Eviction:** no administrative stay found. What Texas gives instead is upstream: the 60-day
  cure period (§ 263.503(c)(18)), the mandatory pre-eviction meeting (§ 263.503(h)(2)), the
  absolute bar on proceeding if the debt is cured (§ 263.503(i)), and the ordinary judicial
  timeline of a forcible detainer suit. Whether a Texas justice-court eviction can be stayed
  (e.g., by appeal bond / pauper's affidavit under TRCP 510.9) was **not researched** and should
  not be asserted from this note.

### Absence findings (deliberate, not gaps)

- **No requirement that a provider's eviction notice state any appeal, hearing, or defense
  right.** Checked 26 TAC §§ 263.501, 263.502, 263.503 (all 22 required agreement contents and
  all of subsections (d) and (h)–(m)), 263.701, 263.707, 263.708, 263.801, and 1 TAC § 357.11.
- **No administrative appeal of an eviction** from an HCS residence. The only administrative
  forum found is the HHSC fair hearing, and its subject matter is the *service* action, not the
  tenancy.
- **No short-notice / emergency eviction exception, and no state agency pre-approval
  requirement** for an eviction. Contrast California's 3-day-with-CCL-approval route.
- **No provider-initiated involuntary discharge pathway** was found outside the two eviction
  grounds in § 263.503(c)(17). 26 TAC § 263.701 is expressly a process for the *individual* to
  transfer, triggered by the individual's or LAR's stated intent.

### Unresolved conflicts and open questions

1. **§ 263.503(c)(7)(B) vs (c)(8).** (c)(7)(B) makes the agreement's own provisions govern over
   conflicting state statutory law "to the extent allowed by law"; (c)(8) forbids waiver of
   tenant rights and shorter notice periods. These pull in opposite directions. No case law or
   HHSC interpretation was located. Do not tell a person their agreement can override the
   Property Code, and do not tell them it never can.
2. **Whether a Texas justice-court eviction can be stayed pending appeal** (appeal bond,
   statement of inability to pay, TRCP 510.9) was **not researched**. Question 6 above is
   answered only as to the administrative stay.
3. **DBMD waiver (26 TAC Ch. 260) and assisted living (26 TAC Ch. 553 / H&S Code Ch. 247)** were
   not researched. Several adults with DD live in those settings. Do not generalize the HCS
   answer to them.
4. **ICF/IID discharge and transfer rights** rest on the adopted federal ICF/IID conditions of
   participation rather than on a Texas rule; the federal side was not researched here.
5. **26 TAC § 565.25** (annual satisfaction assessment, 60-day response) was read only from a
   search snippet. It is characterized above as a quality process rather than an appeal; confirm
   against the full text before relying on that characterization.
