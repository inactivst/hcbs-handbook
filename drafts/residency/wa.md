# Washington (WA) — residency agreement and involuntary discharge

- **State:** Washington
- **Researched:** 2026-07-30
- **Researcher:** agent (RightsBook residency/discharge pass)
- **Method:** `drafts/residency-research-scope.md`, source hierarchy tiers 1–4

## Facility categories — read this before using any answer below

Washington splits provider-owned DD housing across two regulatory stacks, and they
carry different rules. Never move an answer from one column to the other.

| Setting | What it is | Who regulates the housing | Who regulates the service |
|---|---|---|---|
| **DDA group home** | The main provider-owned DD residence. Per `WAC 388-101-3000(17)`, a group home is **licensed as an assisted living facility (ch. 388-78A WAC) or as an adult family home (ch. 388-76 WAC)** and DDA-certified on top. | RCW 70.129 + ch. 388-76 or 388-78A WAC | ch. 388-101D WAC (DDA certification) |
| **Adult family home** | Provider's own home, up to 6 residents. Many DD adults live here. | RCW 70.128, RCW 70.129, ch. 388-76 WAC | ch. 388-101D WAC if DDA-contracted |
| **Group training home** | Certified nonprofit facility under RCW 71A.22. Small category. | ch. 388-101D WAC only (not AFH/ALF licensed) | ch. 388-101D WAC |
| **Supported living** | **Not provider-owned.** `WAC 388-101-3000(49)`: services to "clients living in homes that are owned, rented, or leased by the client or their legal representative." The client is an ordinary tenant. | RCW 59.18 (Residential Landlord-Tenant Act), as a normal tenancy | ch. 388-101D WAC |
| **Enhanced services facility** | ch. 70.97 RCW; behavioral/complex needs. | RCW 70.129, ch. 388-107 WAC | — |

The practical consequence: for most WA adults with DD in provider-owned housing, the
**adult family home / assisted living rules are the operative ones**, and `WAC 388-101D`
sits on top of them governing the *service*, not the *tenancy*.

## The seven answers

### 1. Is a written residency/admission agreement required for provider-owned DD housing? Which regulation?

**Yes — and it is brand new.** Effective **2026-01-01**, Washington requires a signed
written **"residency agreement"** for every resident whose care is paid, wholly or
partly, by Medicaid:

- Adult family homes (incl. DDA group homes with an AFH license): **`WAC 388-76-10506`**
- Assisted living facilities (incl. DDA group homes with an ALF license): **`WAC 388-78A-2651`**
- Enhanced services facilities: **`WAC 388-107-0161`**

All three were filed under `WSR 25-18-037` (8/25/25, eff. 1/1/26) with statutory
authority expressly including **`42 C.F.R. 441.301(c)(4)(vi)(A)`** and `441.530(a)(1)(vi)(A)` —
i.e. Washington adopted these *specifically* to answer the HCBS settings rule. The rule
defines the agreement as "a legally enforceable written document."

**Separately and for all residents regardless of payor**, `RCW 70.129.030` requires a
written notice of rights and services before admission. DSHS is explicit that these are
two different documents (HCBS Implementation FAQ, 2025-08-26: "No. The new residency
agreement is specific to residents whose care is paid for through Medicaid").

**For group training homes only:** `WAC 388-101D-0695(2)` — "Each client must sign a
written agreement with the group training home. The written agreement must include the
client's notice rights for termination of services."

**For supported living:** no state-required residency agreement, because the client holds
their own lease and the ordinary RLTA applies.

**What the residency agreement must contain is narrow.** `WAC 388-76-10506(5)` sets out
the whole form: the facility agrees to comply with RCW 70.129 transfer/discharge rules,
and the resident is told they may have a free lawyer. *It does not require the agreement
to describe an appeal process.* (This is exactly the CA trap in reverse — do not upgrade
"conditions/rights specific to transfer and discharge" into "appeal process.")

### 2. Does state landlord/tenant law apply, or is there a carve-out?

**Split answer, and this is the hinge.**

- **Supported living:** the RLTA applies normally. The client owns/rents/leases the home
  (`WAC 388-101-3000(49)`), so they are a tenant with full ch. 59.18 RCW rights.
- **Adult family homes, assisted living facilities (therefore DDA group homes), nursing
  homes, ESFs:** residents are **not treated as RLTA tenants.** Washington did not answer
  `(vi)(A)` by extending landlord-tenant law to these settings; it answered by creating a
  *parallel* contractual instrument (the residency agreement) plus a right to counsel.
  DSHS says so in its own words — the federal rule requires protections "comparable to
  those provided by the Landlord Tenant Act," and the department's response was the new
  WACs, not RLTA coverage.
- **Group training homes:** eviction runs through **unlawful detainer under ch. 59.12 or
  59.16 RCW** — the general/commercial UD chapters, *not* the RLTA. `WAC 388-101D-0700`.

**Precision warning on the carve-out cite.** A widely repeated line — "This act does not
apply to assisted living facilities licensed under chapter 18.20 RCW, to nursing homes
licensed under chapter 18.51 RCW, to adult family homes licensed under chapter 70.128 RCW,
or to continuing care retirement communities registered under chapter 18.390 RCW" — is an
**application note to 2021 c 115**, displayed under `RCW 59.18.620`. It scopes *that 2021
act*, not the whole RLTA. The general exemption statute, **`RCW 59.18.040`**, does *not*
name AFHs or ALFs; its subsection (1) exempts "[r]esidence at an institution... where
residence is merely incidental to... the provision of medical, religious, educational,
recreational, or similar services including, but not limited to, correctional facilities,
licensed nursing homes, monasteries and convents, and hospitals." Nursing homes are named;
AFHs and ALFs are not. **Recorded as an unresolved conflict** — see Verification notes.

### 3. Notice period(s) before involuntary discharge, and short-notice exceptions

**Two separate clocks, because two separate actions.**

**(a) Ending the TENANCY — AFH / ALF / ESF (so, DDA group homes).** `RCW 70.129.110(4)`:
**at least 30 days** before transfer or discharge. Mirrored for AFHs at
`WAC 388-76-10616(1)` ("a written thirty day notification").

Short-notice exception — "as soon as practicable" — under `RCW 70.129.110(4)(b)` when:
(i) safety of individuals in the facility would be endangered; (ii) health of individuals
would be endangered; (iii) immediate transfer is required by the resident's urgent medical
needs; or (iv) the resident has not resided in the facility for 30 days.

**Who approves the short notice: nobody.** Unlike California's 3-day notice (which needs
advance Community Care Licensing approval), the WA exception is **self-executing by the
facility**. No state pre-approval is required anywhere in RCW 70.129.110 or WAC 388-76-10616.
The only after-the-fact control is that, for Medicaid residents from 2026-01-01, a copy of
the notice must go to the resident's DSHS case manager **and** to the Long-Term Care
Ombudsman within **three calendar days** (`WAC 388-76-10617(3)-(4)`).

Permitted reasons are closed-list, `RCW 70.129.110(1)`: needs can't be met; safety
endangered; health endangered; nonpayment; facility ceases to operate. The facility must
"first attempt through reasonable accommodations to avoid the transfer or discharge"
(`RCW 70.129.110(3)(a)`).

**(b) Ending the SERVICE — DDA-certified provider.** `WAC 388-101D-0200(2)(b)`:
**at least 60 days** before the termination date, written notice to the client, their legal
representative, and DDA. Grounds at (1): provider cannot meet needs; safety endangered;
health endangered; provider ceases to operate. A contracted supported living provider must
also run the client critical case protocol and attend a critical case conference first
(`(2)(a)`). Crisis diversion providers are exempt (`(5)`).

**Immediate suspension, no notice period.** `WAC 388-101D-0201(1)` lets a contracted
supported living provider **immediately** suspend services if it can't safely meet needs,
the client endangers health or safety, **and** the client is in a hospital, jail, health
care facility, or other setting that can address their needs. Written notice must be given
before suspending, but there is no waiting period. No outside approver.

**(c) DDA's own action.** `WAC 388-825-105(1)`: at least **10 days** advance notice to
terminate eligibility or terminate/reduce services. `(2)`: at least **30 days** before
transferring a person from a residential habilitation center to the community.

### 4. Is there an appeal — ADMINISTRATIVE or JUDICIAL?

**Depends entirely on who is doing the ending. Three different forums.**

**(a) Against the PROVIDER's discharge from an AFH / ALF / DDA group home:
no administrative appeal.** There is no fair-hearing right in RCW 70.129, in
ch. 388-76 WAC, or in the new 2026 rules. Chapter 70.129 RCW has **no hearing or appeal
section at all** (sections run .005 through .901; the nearest is `.170`, "Nonjudicial
remedies through regulatory authorities encouraged—Remedies cumulative"). What Washington
gives instead, from 2026-01-01, is a **right to a free lawyer** to contest the discharge —
a statewide "long-term care discharge defense screening line" — which is *legal
representation*, not a forum. The forum, if it comes to one, is **judicial** (defending an
eviction/unlawful detainer) or a complaint to DSHS's Complaint Resolution Unit, which is a
**regulatory complaint, not the resident's appeal**.

  This is a live, documented gap. `RCW 70.129.005` states the legislature's intent "to
  extend those basic rights" — the federal nursing-home rights at 42 U.S.C. 1396r and
  42 C.F.R. part 483, which include a transfer/discharge hearing — "to residents in
  veterans' homes, assisted living facilities, enhanced services facilities, and adult
  family homes." The Washington State Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program states DSHS never
  wrote the rule to implement it (2022-11-08; see Source library). Treat "there is an
  administrative appeal for an AFH/ALF discharge" as **not confirmed** — some legal-aid
  material says an eviction can be appealed to the Office of Administrative Hearings, but
  I could not trace that to any RCW or WAC, and the Ombudsman says the rule was never
  adopted. **Unresolved conflict, flagged below.**

**(b) Against the PROVIDER's termination of DDA-certified services** (`WAC 388-101D-0200`):
**no appeal of any kind is created by the rule.** The rule tells the provider what to
write and who to tell. It does not give the client a hearing.

**(c) Against DDA's decision: yes, ADMINISTRATIVE, and it is strong.**
`RCW 71A.10.050(1)` gives a right to appeal, among others, "(c) A denial, reduction, or
termination of a service" and "(g) A decision to change a person's placement from one
category of residential services to a different category of residential services," with
"[t]he adjudicative proceeding... governed by the Administrative Procedure Act, chapter
34.05 RCW." Implemented at `WAC 388-825-120(2)`; hearing before the Office of
Administrative Hearings; 90 days to file (`WAC 388-825-130(2)`); oral requests accepted
(`WAC 388-825-125(2)`).

  **The catch that matters:** this appeals *DDA's* action. When a private provider issues
  its own 60-day termination notice, the person's route to a hearing is via whatever DDA
  then decides about their services and placement — not a direct appeal against the
  provider.

**(d) Group training homes:** `WAC 388-101D-0700` sends the provider to "[a]pplicable
legal processes, such as unlawful detainer under chapters 59.12 or 59.16 RCW." **Judicial**,
a court defense, closest to the California model.

### 5. Must the NOTICE ITSELF state the appeal right?

**Split — and Washington's answer is unusual and worth quoting.**

**Provider discharge notice (AFH/ALF/ESF): NO appeal right, but YES a stated right to a
free lawyer.** From 2026-01-01, `WAC 388-76-10617(2)` requires the notice to say, in
substantially these words: *"Subject to legislative appropriation you have a right to a
free lawyer to help you respond to a notice of transfer or discharge."* The prescribed
notice form at `(5)(h)` also requires contact details for the Long-Term Care Ombuds,
**Disability Rights Washington** (named in the rule, for people with an intellectual
disability or mental illness), and the DSHS complaint hotline.

  So: Washington is one of the few states where the discharge notice must affirmatively
  point the resident to a lawyer. It is **not** Minnesota — the notice does not state an
  appeal right, because there is no administrative appeal to state. It is a *counsel*
  requirement, not an *appeal* requirement. Do not conflate them.

  The base notice contents (`RCW 70.129.110(5)`, `WAC 388-76-10616(1)`) for **all**
  residents require reason, effective date, destination, the LTC ombuds, and — expressly
  for residents with developmental disabilities — the P&A agency's address and phone.
  **No appeal right is listed.**

**Provider service-termination notice (DDA, `WAC 388-101D-0200(3)`): NO.** The required
contents are the reason, the circumstances that led to it, the steps taken to prevent it,
and the effective date. **No appeal right, no ombuds, no P&A, no legal resources.** This
is the weakest notice in the Washington stack and the cleanest single ask for reform.

**DDA's own notice: YES.** `WAC 388-825-104(4)` — the notice from DDA "will include...
(4) Appeal rights to the decision." `WAC 388-825-125(1)` adds that the notice includes how
and where to file and the deadline, and `WAC 388-825-130(4)` that it states the date the
ten-day notice period ends. `RCW 71A.10.050(3)`: "The notice must include a statement
advising the recipient of the right to an adjudicative proceeding and the time limits for
filing an application for an adjudicative proceeding."

### 6. Is there a stay / "services continue pending appeal"?

**Yes — but only against DDA, not against a provider.**

`WAC 388-825-145(2)`: if you request a hearing on DDA's decision to reduce or terminate
services **before the date specified on the notice**, and no `WAC 388-825-150` exception
applies, "the department will not reduce or terminate those services unless and until an
administrative law judge issues an initial order or a review judge issues a final order
that reduces or terminates those services."

`WAC 388-825-145(3)` is the placement-specific one and is the strongest sentence in the
Washington set: "The department will take no action until there is a final decision on your
appeal of the department's decision to remove or transfer you to another residential
service unless one or more of the conditions in WAC 388-825-150 applies."

Conditions that defeat the stay (`WAC 388-825-150`) include: health, safety or wellbeing in
imminent jeopardy `(7)`; the provider is no longer qualified — no contract, decertification,
license revoked or suspended `(8)`; late hearing request `(10)`. Deadline trap: to keep
services you must file **within the ten-day notice period**, not the 90-day filing window
(`WAC 388-825-130(3)`). And `WAC 388-825-145(5)` — you may have to pay back up to 60 days
of continued benefits if you lose.

RHC-to-community transfers get an even firmer stay plus a burden shift onto the department
(`WAC 388-825-155(3)-(5)`; `RCW 71A.10.050(2)`).

**No stay against a provider's discharge.** Nothing in RCW 70.129, ch. 388-76 WAC, or
`WAC 388-101D-0200` pauses a provider's 30-day or 60-day notice while anything is contested.
**Absence recorded as a finding.**

### Unresolved conflicts — do not paper over these

1. **Is there an administrative hearing against an AFH/ALF discharge?** Legal-aid material
   (Northwest Justice Project / WashingtonLawHelp, blocked) says yes, before an ALJ at the
   Office of Administrative Hearings. The Washington State LTC Ombudsman says DSHS never
   adopted the rule. I found **no RCW or WAC creating it**: ch. 70.129 RCW has no hearing
   section, ch. 388-76 WAC has none, and the 2025 rulemaking answered the HCBS settings
   rule with a right to counsel rather than a hearing — which is strong circumstantial
   evidence that no hearing right exists. **Recorded as: no administrative appeal
   confirmed. Requires a human to open the WashingtonLawHelp page and, if it cites
   something, chase that cite.**

2. **Exact statutory basis for AFH/ALF exclusion from the RLTA.** The commonly quoted
   sentence is an application note to 2021 c 115 and scopes only that act (entry 21).
   `RCW 59.18.040` names licensed nursing homes but not AFHs or ALFs (entry 20). The
   practical exclusion is well corroborated — DSHS built a parallel "comparable to the
   Landlord Tenant Act" instrument rather than extending the RLTA, and `RCW 18.20.020`
   reportedly excludes RLTA-governed housing from the ALF definition (search-corroborated,
   not fetched). But **I could not find a clean single statute saying "the RLTA does not
   apply to adult family homes."** State the practical position, not a fake cite.

3. **Group training home eviction chapter.** `WAC 388-101D-0700` points at ch. 59.12/59.16
   RCW (unlawful detainer / forcible entry), not ch. 59.18 RCW. Whether a group training
   home resident is nonetheless an RLTA tenant is not resolved by the rule text.

### Things checked and genuinely absent (absence as finding)
- No requirement anywhere that an AFH/ALF/ESF discharge notice state an **appeal** right.
  What it must state, from 2026-01-01, is a right to a **free lawyer**.
- No requirement that the `WAC 388-101D-0200` provider service-termination notice mention
  the ombudsman, Disability Rights Washington, legal resources, or any appeal.
- No stay or "services continue" mechanism against a **provider's** discharge or service
  termination. The stay in `WAC 388-825-145` runs only against **DDA**.
- No state pre-approval requirement for a short-notice discharge (contrast California's
  3-day notice, which needs Community Care Licensing approval in advance).
