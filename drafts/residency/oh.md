# Ohio — residency agreement and involuntary discharge

- **State:** Ohio (OH)
- **Retrieval date:** 2026-07-30
- **Researcher:** agent (RightsBook residency/discharge pass)
- **Method:** `drafts/residency-research-scope.md`
- **Headline:** Ohio is a strong state on this topic. Administrative appeal, the notice
  itself must state the appeal right, and services continue pending the hearing — but only
  in **licensed residential facilities**, and none of it applies in an emergency.

## Setting categories — read this before using any answer below

Ohio's rules split by category, and the split changes the answer completely.

| Category | Definition source | Written agreement | Discharge rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Licensed residential facility** (group home; includes a non-department-operated ICF/IID) | ORC 5123.19(A)(5); OAC 5123-9-02(B)(19) | Residency agreement — OAC 5123-9-02(G)(2) | **OAC 5123-3-05** (30 days, administrative appeal, stay) |
| **Department-operated ICF/IID** (developmental centers) | ORC 5123.19 | not researched | **Expressly excluded** from OAC 5123-3-05(A) |
| **Shared living setting**, or a home owned by a non-related independent provider who lives there ("provider-owned") | OAC 5123-9-02(B)(15)(a),(b) | Residency agreement — OAC 5123-9-02(G)(2) | Contractual: 30 days without cause / 5 days with cause, OAC 5123-9-02(G)(2)(j). **No 5123-3-05 appeal.** |
| **Provider-controlled residential setting** (landlord affiliated with, but not, the provider) | OAC 5123-9-02(B)(14) | **Lease** — OAC 5123-9-02(G)(1); "lease" = a Chapter 5321 rental agreement, (B)(12) | Ordinary Ohio landlord/tenant + eviction. **No 5123-3-05 appeal.** |
| Supported living / independent living arrangement | ORC 5123.19(A)(1), (A)(5)(b)(iv) | not a residential facility | Provider may cease **services** on 30 days' notice, OAC 5123-2-08(G)(8) / 5123-2-09(F)(3) |

Everything strong in Ohio sits in the first row only.

## 1. Is a written residency/admission agreement required?

**Yes — but it comes from the HCBS settings rule, not the licensing chapter.**

OAC **5123-9-02(G)(2)** requires a written **residency agreement**, consented to by both the
individual and the landlord, when a person lives in a licensed residential facility, or in a
shared living setting or an independent provider's own home where the provider is not related
to the person. OAC **5123-9-02(G)(1)** requires a **lease** instead in a provider-*controlled*
residential setting.

The required contents are long and specific (5123-9-02(G)(2)(a)-(k)): landlord contact,
disclosure that the setting is a residential facility or shared living setting, the
landlord/provider relationship and whether the person may choose a different HCBS provider,
landlord maintenance and access obligations, and the HCBS-rule rights — choose roommates, lock
on the bedroom door, decorate, visitors at any time, control own schedule, access food at any
time. Rent must be "reasonable and comparable to community standards" and set on the
accommodations, "not upon an individual's assets, resources, or ability to pay".

**What the agreement must say about ending it.** For a licensed residential facility the
agreement must contain "a statement that the residential facility will terminate services in
accordance with rule 5123-3-05 of the Administrative Code" — 5123-9-02(G)(2)(k). It does not
itself have to spell out the appeal steps; it points at the rule that carries them. (This is
exactly the pilot's California trap: the agreement states the *conditions*, the appeal lives
in a different instrument. In Ohio the appeal is real, it is just not written into the
agreement text.)

**Absence finding:** the licensing chapter's own admission rule, OAC **5123-3-04(C)**, requires
only informed consent ("Admission to a residential facility is voluntary, requiring informed
consent") and written notice of the admission decision within 30 days. It contains no
residency-agreement requirement. The DD rights statute, **ORC 5123.62**, lists 24 rights and
says nothing about housing agreements, notice, or discharge.

## 2. Does state landlord/tenant law apply, or is there a carve-out?

**No express carve-out for DD facilities — but Ohio routes only one category into Chapter 5321
by name, and leaves the rest ambiguous.**

- **No statutory carve-out.** ORC **5321.01(C)** excludes ten kinds of occupancy from
  "residential premises." Chapters 5123 and 5119 are **not** among them. The list names jails
  and halfway houses, "[h]ospitals and similar institutions with the primary purpose of
  providing medical services, and homes licensed pursuant to Chapter 3721. of the Revised Code"
  (nursing homes), transient lodging, boarding schools, orphanages, farm residences, Chapter
  3733 units, owner-occupied condominiums, certain SRO facilities, and 501(c)(3) emergency
  shelters. A DD group home licensed under ORC 5123.19 is not excluded on the face of the
  statute.
- **One narrow carve-out that can touch DD occupancy:** 5321.01(C)(9)(b)(i) excludes occupancy
  in a **licensed SRO facility** owned or operated by a 501(c)(3), where the occupancy is for
  participation in a program providing services "for the rehabilitation of persons with mental
  illnesses, persons with developmental disabilities..." That is an SRO-specific exclusion, not
  a group-home exclusion.
- **Where Ohio affirmatively applies Chapter 5321:** in a provider-*controlled* residential
  setting, OAC 5123-9-02(G)(1) requires a lease, and (B)(12) defines "lease" as "a written
  rental agreement meeting the requirements for rental agreements set forth in Chapter 5321. of
  the Revised Code." Full landlord/tenant law, judicial eviction under ORC Chapter 1923.
- **Where it is left open:** in a licensed residential facility or shared living setting, the
  required instrument is a "residency agreement," defined at 5123-9-02(B)(18) **without** any
  reference to Chapter 5321. Ohio built a parallel, rule-created instrument there rather than
  declaring those residents tenants.

**Could not confirm:** whether an Ohio court would treat a licensed-residential-facility
resident as a "tenant" under ORC 5321.01(A) entitled to Chapter 5321 and Chapter 1923
protections. No statute answers it and no case law was located within this pass. Treat this as
open; do not tell anyone they are or are not a tenant on the strength of this note.

## 3. Notice period(s) and short-notice exception

**Licensed residential facility — 30 calendar days, no exception approval required.**

OAC 5123-3-05(C)(4): notice "must be made at least thirty calendar days before the transfer or
termination of services, except when an emergency exists."

"Emergency" is defined at 5123-3-05(B)(4) and has two branches: (a) despite documented attempts
to provide, obtain and/or coordinate services, a significant risk of substantial harm remains
that cannot be met in the current environment "such that action must be taken immediately"; or
(b) a level-of-care determination or preadmission screening shows the person needs a level of
services provided in another type of setting.

**Who approves the short notice: nobody, in advance.** Unlike California's 3-day notice, which
requires prior Community Care Licensing approval, the Ohio rule places the emergency
determination with the **operator**. There is no pre-approval by DODD, the county board, or a
hearing officer. Control is retrospective only — the operator can face adverse licensing action
under OAC 5123-3-06 for not following the rule (5123-3-05(G)), and the hearing officer places
the burden of proof on the operator (5123-3-05(F)(6)). This is the weakest joint in an
otherwise strong scheme, because the emergency finding also switches off the stay in (C)(5).

A partial safeguard: 5123-3-05(C)(4) second sentence — if before the 30 days run out the
operator determines the emergency conditions no longer exist, "the subject of the transfer or
termination of services may return to the residential facility."

**Grounds are limited.** 5123-3-05(C)(1) says the operator "will allow each resident to remain"
and shall not initiate a transfer or termination unless one of six listed conditions applies
(needs can no longer be met without undue hardship; resident no longer needs/wants the services
or chooses to move; significant risk of substantial harm to self, others, or staff; nonpayment
including by Medicaid or another third-party payer; permanent capacity reduction; the facility
ceases to operate).

**Other categories.**
- Shared living / independent-provider-owned home: the residency agreement must state the
  landlord may terminate it "[w]ithout cause upon thirty-day advance written notice" or "[w]ith
  cause upon five-day advance written notice" — OAC 5123-9-02(G)(2)(j). Mirror rights run to the
  individual under (G)(2)(i).
- Ending **services** (any certified provider, any setting): 30 calendar days' written notice to
  the individual and the service and support administrator — OAC 5123-2-08(G)(8) for agency
  providers, 5123-2-09(F)(3) for independent providers. The independent-provider rule has an
  immediate-notification carve-out when the provider's own health or safety "is at immediate
  risk of serious harm." Both rules also require the provider, on realizing it may be unable to
  continue, to "immediately engage" the person and the SSA about alternatives.

## 4. Is there an appeal, and is it administrative or judicial?

**ADMINISTRATIVE**, and unusually complete — for licensed residential facilities only. It is a
four-stage ladder under OAC 5123-3-05(D)-(F):

1. **Internal appeal** — in writing to the facility's governing board or administrator within
   **7 calendar days** of receiving the notice. (D)(1). The board must review and answer in
   writing by certified mail within **5 calendar days**, and a denial must include the DODD
   director's contact information and an explanation, in a manner the individual understands,
   of the 5-day window to appeal onward. (D)(2).
2. **Appeal to the DODD director** — in writing within **5 calendar days** of the board's
   notice. (D)(3). "Upon request, department staff will assist the individual to understand the
   appeal process and find resources to help the individual with preparation of the appeal."
3. **Mandatory mediation** — within **15 calendar days** of receipt of the appeal, mediator
   supplied by DODD, nonbinding unless all parties agree in writing, and **legal representation
   is not permitted**. (E)(1)-(6).
4. **Administrative hearing** — before a DODD-appointed hearing officer (not the mediator),
   within **30 calendar days** of the end of mediation. (F)(3)-(4). Both sides present evidence;
   the individual may have legal representation and/or an authorized representative. (F)(5).
   **The burden of proof is on the operator** to show the transfer or termination complied with
   the rule. (F)(6). Written recommendation to the director within 10 days; the director issues
   a written decision within **5 calendar days** and "may impose conditions on the transfer or
   termination of services." (F)(8)-(9).

This is a **state agency fair-hearing-style process**, not a court defense and not merely an
internal provider grievance — though stage 1 *is* an internal grievance, and it is a mandatory
gate before the agency ever sees the case. Say "internal review first, then a DODD hearing."

**Judicial:** the rule creates no court route. An ordinary appeal of a director's order would
run through ORC Chapter 119 / 2506 principles; **not verified in this pass**, do not assert it.

**Absence finding for the other categories:** no equivalent administrative appeal was found for
shared living, independent-provider-owned homes, or provider-controlled leases. There the
remedy is contractual/judicial — Chapter 5321 and a Chapter 1923 forcible entry and detainer
action for leases, and for a residency agreement, whatever a court makes of the contract.

## 5. Must the notice itself state the appeal right?

**YES.** This is Ohio's headline, and it is the exact ask from the Minnesota comparison.

OAC 5123-3-05(C)(3)(a) requires written notice by certified mail to both the individual and the
county board, and that notice "will contain" five items, including **(iv) "The individual's
right to appeal the transfer or termination of services and the process to do so"** and
**(v) "The telephone number and address of disability rights Ohio"** — the state's P&A, named
in the rule.

Ohio then goes one step past a paper requirement. 5123-3-05(C)(3)(b) requires the operator to
"[e]xplain the transfer or termination of services and appeal rights to the individual in a
manner the individual understands." A written notice plus a required accessible oral
explanation is a stronger construction than Minnesota's notice-contents list alone.

The chain repeats at the next stage: a denial at internal review must itself carry the
director's contact information and an understandable explanation of the 5-day appeal window.
(D)(2).

## 6. Is there a stay / services continue pending appeal?

**YES, and it is automatic on request — with one large hole.**

OAC 5123-3-05(C)(5): "If an individual requests a hearing regarding the transfer or termination
of services, the residential facility must maintain services or the availability of services
until a decision is rendered after the hearing unless an emergency exists."

Two things to say precisely:

- **Stronger than Minnesota in form.** Minnesota requires the notice to tell the person they may
  "seek a temporary order staying the termination" — the person must ask an adjudicator for it.
  Ohio's continuation attaches on the request for a hearing itself. No motion, no separate order.
- **Weaker in reach.** It switches off "unless an emergency exists," and the emergency is
  declared by the operator (see Q3). The cases with the shortest notice are the same cases with
  no stay. And 5123-3-05(C)(6): if the person waives the right to appeal in writing after
  receiving the notice, "the operator is not required to maintain services or the availability
  of services" — a written waiver presented at a bad moment ends the protection.

**Absence finding:** no stay mechanism was found for shared living, independent-provider-owned,
or provider-controlled settings.

**Unresolved conflicts / oddities:**
1. **Stale internal cross-reference.** OAC 5123-9-02(B)(11) defines "landlord" by reference to
   "a lease or a residency agreement meeting the requirements set forth in paragraph (F) of this
   rule," but the lease and residency agreement requirements are in **paragraph (G)**. Apparently
   a cross-reference not updated in the Jan. 26, 2024 amendment. Cite (G), and note the glitch if
   anyone points at (F).
2. **Old rule numbers still circulating.** Ohio renumbered `5123:2-x-xx` to `5123-x-xx`. Search
   engines still return `5123:2-3-05` ("admission, termination of services, and transfer"). The
   current rule is **5123-3-05** and it dropped "admission" from the title, because admission
   moved to 5123-3-04. Anything citing 5123:2-3-05 is citing a superseded version.
3. **"Provider-owned" vs "provider-controlled"** are separate defined terms in Ohio with
   different requirements (5123-9-02(B)(14) vs (B)(15)). Do not use them interchangeably; the
   federal phrase "provider-owned or controlled" maps to both.
