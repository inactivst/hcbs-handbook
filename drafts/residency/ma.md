# Massachusetts — residency agreements and involuntary discharge from provider-owned DD housing

- **State:** Massachusetts (MA)
- **Researched:** 2026-07-30
- **Researcher:** agent (RightsBook residency/discharge pass)
- **Primary agency:** Department of Developmental Services (DDS), regulations at 115 CMR
- **Facility categories covered here:** DDS-licensed / DDS-funded 24-hour community residential
  supports (group homes) and DDS "facilities" (the state-operated developmental centers /
  former state schools, defined at M.G.L. c. 123B, § 1). Shared living and Adult Foster Care
  (AFC) are flagged separately where the rule differs. Nursing facilities and DMH-licensed
  residences run on entirely different rules and are **excluded** — see Verification notes.

---

## The short version

Massachusetts is unusual: it did **not** build a residency-agreement carve-out, because the state's
official position is that ordinary landlord/tenant law already applies to these homes. On top of
that baseline it layers a **separate administrative track — the DDS "Transfer Statute"** — that
governs the state moving a person from one home to another. That track has three things most
states lack: a **45-day written notice**, a **notice that must state the person's rights**, and a
**hard stay: the transfer cannot happen while the appeal is pending.**

The gap is on the other axis. The Transfer Statute regulates a *transfer between DDS facilities/homes*.
It does not, by its terms, regulate a **provider unilaterally ending its own service** to a person.
That falls back to the ISP-modification and ISP-appeal machinery at 115 CMR 6.20–6.34, where the
stay is much weaker.

---

## 1. Is a written residency/admission agreement required for provider-owned DD housing? Which regulation?

**Qualified yes — but it is not required by 115 CMR.** There is no DDS regulation that says
"the provider shall execute a residency agreement." The requirement reaches Massachusetts providers
through the federal HCBS Community Rule, 42 CFR 441.301(c)(4)(vi)(A), as implemented by:

- **DDS Office of Quality Management Memorandum, "Guidance on Residential Agreements and the
  CMS HCBS Waiver 'Community Rule,'" January 24, 2023** (tier 2), which "is intended to assist all
  DDS Providers who provide 24-hour residential supports in complying with the Community Rule," and
- the **DDS Licensure and Certification tool**, into which the Statewide Transition Plan says DDS
  "incorporat[ed] … requirements for residency agreements … August 2016 — Complete."

The 2023 guidance's operative instruction is that the *default* is a real tenancy: providers are
told to "refer to sources of Massachusetts landlord tenant law … See M.G.L. c. 239 and c. 186, § 11."
A "residency agreement" is the **fallback**, used only where the provider determines landlord/tenant
law does not apply — and in that case the guidance says the agreement "should, at a minimum,
incorporate reference to the individual's rights under the DDS 'Transfer Statute,' M.G.L. c. 123B, § 3."

**Note on the trap:** 115 CMR 3.04 ("Admission to and Discharge from the Facilities") *does* exist,
but it governs only the state-operated facilities listed at 115 CMR 3.03 (Wrentham, Hogan, Templeton,
etc.), and all it requires is that "[t]he Department shall maintain a written policy on the standards
and procedures for admission to and discharge from the facilities listed in 115 CMR 3.03." That is
**not** a community group-home residency-agreement rule and must not be cited as one.

**Adult Foster Care is a different category.** AFC is a MassHealth state plan service, not a DDS
waiver service, governed by 130 CMR 408.435(A); MassHealth handles AFC Community Rule compliance
with its own attestation form, lease guidance, and residency agreement template.

## 2. Does state landlord/tenant law apply, or is there a carve-out?

**It applies. Massachusetts asserts no carve-out — this is the hinge and MA answers it on the
"landlord/tenant law applies" side of 441.301(c)(4)(vi)(A).**

The Statewide Transition Plan states flatly that "DDS transfer statute/regulations are more robust
than Massachusetts landlord tenant law, and MA landlord/tenant law is considered one of the most
'tenant friendly' states in the country. See 115 CMR 6.63." The 2023 DDS guidance sends providers
to M.G.L. c. 239 (summary process) and c. 186, § 11.

So the baseline eviction remedy in a MA provider-owned DD home is **judicial**: a summary process
action in Housing Court under c. 239, preceded by a statutory notice to quit under c. 186. For a
tenancy at will that is **30 days or one full rental period, whichever is longer** (c. 186, § 12),
or **14 days for nonpayment of rent** (c. 186, §§ 11, 12).

Caveat the 2023 guidance itself flags: whether a given individual actually holds a tenancy is a
fact question the provider is told to put to counsel, and the guidance identifies real cases where
the answer is no — e.g. public or subsidized housing already under a lease or management agreement
between a housing agency and the provider "and the lease or management agreement does not permit a
sublease or subrogation to the individual." Where that is true, the residency-agreement fallback
plus the Transfer Statute is what the person has.

## 3. Notice period(s) before involuntary discharge, and short-notice exception plus who approves it

Three separate tracks. Keep them apart.

### (a) DDS transfer between homes — M.G.L. c. 123B, § 3 and 115 CMR 6.63
- **45 days' written notice** before the proposed transfer, to all parties eligible to participate
  in the ISP modification meeting; delivered to the individual and sent by **registered mail** to
  the guardian and other parties. (115 CMR 6.63(2), (2)(a); c. 123B, § 3.)
- **Short-notice exception — non-emergency.** The 45 days "may be shortened to a reasonable period
  under the circumstances **by the service coordinator at his or her discretion**," and only on
  request of (or, in a narrow case, with the non-objection of) the individual, family, designated
  representative, or permanent guardian. 115 CMR 6.63(2)(d)1.–4. **Approver: the service coordinator.**
- **Emergency exception.** Where the transfer responds to "an emergency involving a serious or
  immediate threat to the health or safety of the individual or others," the transfer "may be made
  immediately," with telephone or other notice to the individual and guardian/representative/family
  **within eight hours after the transfer** and full written notice to all parties on the next
  business day. 115 CMR 6.63(5); c. 123B, § 3 (final paragraph). The statute additionally bars
  emergency transfer of a person with an intellectual disability **to a facility for the mentally
  ill**. The 45-day objection clock still runs from the after-the-fact notice.
- Several things are expressly **not** transfers and get no 45-day notice: internal moves within a
  facility or residential location; relocation of the whole home due to lease termination or
  foreclosure; a change in the identity of the provider under a contract award involving no move
  (115 CMR 6.63(1)(a)–(b)). In the contract/procurement cases the service coordinator must still
  convene an information-sharing meeting, "[i]f possible … in advance of the change in residence"
  (115 CMR 6.25(8)) — a meeting, not a notice period, and no appeal attaches.

### (b) Ending or reducing the SERVICE (as opposed to moving the person)
- For most DDS DD adults this is an **ISP modification** under 115 CMR 6.25, which carries only a
  **10-day notice of the modification meeting** (115 CMR 6.25(4)(c)) and then written notice of the
  decision within 10 days after the meeting (6.25(7)). There is **no 30/60/90-day service-termination
  notice period of the Minnesota 245D.10 type in Massachusetts DDS regulation — could not confirm one,
  and I do not believe one exists.**
- For the two DDS-administered waivers where DDS is a MassHealth "acting entity" — **ABI-RH and
  MFP-RS only** — a termination is an appealable action requiring **timely and adequate notice**
  under 130 CMR 610.015 and 610.026.

### (c) Eviction as a tenancy action
- c. 186, § 12: **30 days or one rental period, whichever is longer**, for a tenancy at will.
- c. 186, §§ 11–12: **14 days** for nonpayment.
- Then a c. 239 summary process case in court. Approver: none — this is the landlord's own act,
  tested by a judge afterward.

## 4. Is there an appeal, and is it ADMINISTRATIVE or JUDICIAL?

**Massachusetts has both, on different tracks. The transfer track is genuinely ADMINISTRATIVE, and
it is unusually strong.**

- **Transfer → ADMINISTRATIVE, before a neutral outside agency.** A written objection goes to the
  DDS Commissioner; if the ISP cannot be implemented as a result, **DDS must itself file** for an
  adjudicatory proceeding with the **Division of Administrative Law Appeals (DALA)** within 20 days,
  conducted under M.G.L. c. 30A. **The burden of proof is on the Department**, not on the person.
  DALA is a separate agency from DDS — this is not an in-house hearing officer. (c. 123B, § 3;
  115 CMR 6.63(3)–(4).) Judicial review of the DALA decision lies in Superior Court within 20 days.
- **ISP / service disputes → ADMINISTRATIVE, but in-house.** 115 CMR 6.30–6.34: informal conference
  before the Regional Director or an ombudsperson, then a **fair hearing** before a hearing officer
  who "may be an employee of the Department," decided finally by the Commissioner, with c. 30A, § 14
  judicial review. Here the **burden of proof is on the appellant** (115 CMR 6.34(2)), the mirror
  image of the transfer track. Note 115 CMR 6.30(3): the ISP appeal rules "shall not apply to appeals
  of transfers, which are governed by 115 CMR 6.63" — the two do not overlap.
- **Eviction → JUDICIAL.** Defense of a c. 239 summary process action in Housing Court.
- **ABI-RH / MFP-RS service termination → ADMINISTRATIVE**, a MassHealth Board of Hearings fair
  hearing under 130 CMR 610.000.
- **Not counted here:** the DDS human rights committee process (115 CMR 3.09) is an internal
  complaint mechanism, not an appeal of a discharge, and should not be described as one.

## 5. Must the NOTICE ITSELF state the appeal right?

**YES — and Massachusetts requires it on both DDS tracks. This is the strongest finding in this state.**

- **Transfer notice.** 115 CMR 6.63(2)(c) lists six mandatory contents; item 6 is that the written
  notice shall "include a statement of the rights of the parties established by 115 CMR 6.63, if
  applicable." The statute says the same of the consent request: it "shall include … (3) a statement
  of the rights of said guardian established by this section." (c. 123B, § 3.)
- **ISP modification notice.** 115 CMR 6.25(7): after the Area or Facility Director approves or
  disapproves a modification, "[t]he service coordinator shall notify the team members invited to
  participate in the modification meeting of the decision on the requested modification **and of their
  right to appeal the modification** to the extent provided by 115 CMR 6.63 or 115 CMR 6.30 through
  6.34, as applicable." Because a change in the individual's residence between DDS/provider homes
  **is** a modification (115 CMR 6.25(2)(e)), this notice duty attaches to residential moves too.
- **MassHealth track (ABI-RH / MFP-RS).** 130 CMR 610.026(A)(4)–(5): adequate notice must contain
  "an explanation of the right to request a fair hearing" **and** "the circumstances under which
  assistance is continued if a hearing is requested." 610.031(A) repeats the duty affirmatively.

**Honest limits.** Two gaps worth naming rather than papering over. First, the c. 123B, § 3
rights-statement runs to the **guardian**; it is 115 CMR 6.63(2)(c) that extends the notice contents
to the individual and the other parties. Second, **no Massachusetts source found requires the
*provider's own* notice — a notice to quit, or a letter saying "we are ending your services" — to
state any appeal right.** The notice duties above bind DDS and its service coordinators. A c. 186
notice to quit must carry a nonpayment cure notification (c. 186, § 12) but nothing about appeals.

## 6. Is there a stay / "services continue pending appeal"?

**YES on the transfer track — a real, automatic, statutory stay. Weaker elsewhere.**

- **Transfer, statute:** "During the pendency of said hearing the proposed residential transfer
  shall not occur." (c. 123B, § 3.) No motion required; it is automatic on a timely written objection.
- **Transfer, regulation:** 115 CMR 6.63(4)(b) says the same and goes further — if **DDS wins** at
  DALA it "shall further delay implementation of the proposed transfer for 20 calendar days to enable
  the objecting party to appeal and seek a further stay of the transfer before a judge of the Superior
  Court"; if **DDS loses**, it "shall not proceed with the proposed transfer unless the administrative
  hearing officer's decision is reversed on appeal." Also 6.63(3): where an objection is filed, "the
  transfer shall not occur unless" one of four things happens.
- **Limit:** the stay does not reach the emergency transfer at 6.63(5) / c. 123B, § 3 — an emergency
  transfer happens first and is contested after.
- **ISP appeals — partial stay only.** 115 CMR 6.34(4): portions of the ISP under appeal "shall not
  be implemented **until after the informal conference**," not through the fair hearing, and not at
  all "where earlier implementation is necessary to respond to a serious or immediate threat to the
  health or safety of the individual or others."
- **MassHealth track (ABI-RH / MFP-RS) — full aid-pending.** 130 CMR 610.036(A): assistance
  "will be continued until the BOH decides the appeal" if the request arrives before the
  implementation date, with reinstatement if requested within ten days of the mailing. Subject to
  recoupment under 610.036(D).
- **Eviction track — no administrative stay.** Possession does not change until a court issues an
  execution, but that is the ordinary operation of summary process, not a disability-specific stay.

### Unresolved conflicts and open questions

1. **Does 115 CMR 6.63 actually reach a private provider's group home?** 6.63(1) applies to a move
   from a home "operated or licensed by the Department, or funded through a residential services
   contract other than a master services agreement that was negotiated and executed by the Department."
   That is broad and plainly covers ordinary DDS-funded group homes. But the underlying **statute**,
   c. 123B, § 3, speaks of a transfer "from one residential facility for the mentally retarded to
   another," and the DDS 2023 guidance frames the Transfer Statute as applying where "the setting is a
   DDS 'facility,' as defined in M.G.L. c. 123B, § 2." **c. 123B, § 2 contains no definition of
   "facility" — it is a regulations-authority section.** The definition is in **c. 123B, § 1**:
   "'Facility', a public or private facility for the care and treatment of persons with an intellectual
   disability." That definition is wide enough to include a private group home, and the regulation is
   written to match. **The guidance's internal cite to § 2 is an error; read it as § 1.** Flagging this
   rather than resolving it silently, because an advocate quoting the guidance's § 2 cite will be
   pointed at the wrong section.
2. **Who the notice runs to.** The statute's notice/consent/objection rights are the **guardian's**
   (or nearest relative's). It is the regulation, 115 CMR 6.63(2) and (2)(c), that extends written
   notice to the individual and to all parties eligible to attend the modification meeting, and
   6.63(3) that lets "any party to the ISP modification, other than a current or proposed provider"
   file the objection. A person **without** a guardian is protected by the regulation, not by the
   statute's plain text. Worth naming before telling someone the statute protects them directly.
3. **The escape hatches in 115 CMR 6.25(2)(e) and 6.63(1)(b) are large.** A move caused by competitive
   procurement, contract termination, lease termination, foreclosure, or an unsafe condition of the
   residence is expressly **not** a modification and **not** a transfer — so no 45-day notice, no
   objection, no DALA, no stay. All the person gets is an information-sharing meeting under
   115 CMR 6.25(8), held in advance only "[i]f possible." A provider closing a home for business reasons
   may land squarely in this exception. This is the real gap in the Massachusetts scheme and should be
   said plainly in any chunk written from this file.
4. **P&A source not used.** The Disability Law Center (MA P&A) publishes housing self-advocacy
   materials, but nothing found addressed DDS group-home discharge or the Transfer Statute
   specifically. Tiers 1 and 2 answered every question, so per the hierarchy I stopped there.
5. **Category discipline.** Search results repeatedly surfaced **DMH** residential eviction procedures
   (MassLegalHelp), **assisted living** rules (940 CMR 40, and the Executive Office of Elder Affairs),
   and **nursing facility** transfer/discharge with its own stay at 130 CMR 610.028–610.030. None of
   those govern DDS group homes and **none were used**. The nursing-facility stay at 130 CMR 610.030 is
   a particularly tempting false positive — it is a nursing facility rule only.
