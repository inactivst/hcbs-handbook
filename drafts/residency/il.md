# Illinois — residency agreement and involuntary discharge

- **State:** Illinois (IL)
- **Retrieval date:** 2026-07-30
- **Researcher:** agent
- **Primary facility category researched:** Community-Integrated Living Arrangement (CILA),
  licensed by IDHS under the Community-Integrated Living Arrangements Licensure and
  Certification Act [210 ILCS 135], rules at **59 Ill. Adm. Code 115**. This is where most
  DD adults in provider-owned housing in Illinois live.
- **Second category, tracked separately:** ID/DD facility (ICF/DD), 210 ILCS 47. **CILA is
  expressly excluded from that Act** — see the category-boundary note below. Do not mix them.
- **Cross-cutting rule:** 59 Ill. Adm. Code 120 (Medicaid HCBS Waiver Program for Individuals
  with Developmental Disabilities). Nearly every CILA resident is a DDD Waiver participant, so
  Part 120 supplies the notice period, the appeal path, and the stay that Part 115 does not.

Headline: **Illinois is closer to the Minnesota model than the California model.** It has a
two-step administrative appeal, the notice itself must state the appeal right, and the appeal
automatically stays the termination. It is unusually strong on questions 4, 5 and 6.

---

## 1. Is a written residency/admission agreement required? Which regulation?

**Yes — direct-fetch, tier 1 plus tier 2.**

Two layers say so:

- **59 Ill. Adm. Code 115.200(d)(1)** (CILA rule) requires a provider-owned or controlled
  living arrangement to "Be a specific physical place that can be owned, rented, or occupied
  under a legally enforceable agreement."
- **59 Ill. Adm. Code 120.70(e)(6)(A)** (DDD Waiver rule) carries identical language for
  provider-owned or -controlled residential settings.
- **IDHS Information Bulletin DD.23.008** (tier 2, the operative implementation) makes it
  explicit and names the settings: "When a residential setting is owned or controlled by a
  provider agency, as defined above, the provider must ensure a residency agreement is in
  place for each individual served in the setting. This applies to the following residential
  settings: CILA / CLF, 16 beds or less / CGH."

**Required contents** come from DD.23.008, not from the administrative code. The bulletin lists
five mandatory components, two of which matter here:

- "Statement that the residential provider may end the residency agreement in accordance with
  59 IAC 115 under which a provider can initiate an involuntary termination of the agreement."
- "The individual's appeal rights information Rights of Individuals form [IL462-1201]."

Note the trap the pilot warned about, and note that **Illinois clears it**: California's
`22 C.C.R. § 80068(c)(7)` only requires the agreement to state the conditions under which it can
be ended. Illinois requires that **and** appeal-rights information in the agreement itself.

IDHS publishes a fill-in **Residency Agreement Template** for CILA/CLF/CGH; providers may use
their own, but "the agreement must include all components of the Division's residency agreement
template."

## 2. Does state landlord/tenant law apply, or is there a carve-out?

**Partly confirmed. No carve-out found; the state rule incorporates the tenant standard by
reference rather than answering the question. Direct-fetch, tier 1.**

Illinois copied `42 CFR 441.301(c)(4)(vi)(A)` into its own administrative code nearly verbatim,
at both 115.200(d)(1) and 120.70(e)(6)(A). The operative sentence:

> "The individual shall have, at a minimum, the same responsibilities and protections from
> eviction that tenants have under the landlord/tenant law of the State, county, city, or other
> designated entity."

with the standard fallback for settings where those laws do not apply. So Illinois takes the
position that either landlord/tenant law applies, or the residency agreement must supply
"protections that address eviction processes and appeals comparable" to it.

Two things I could **not** confirm and that a human should not paper over:

- **Whether an Illinois court would treat a CILA resident as a tenant** under the Eviction
  Article, 735 ILCS 5/Art. IX. I found no statutory exemption of CILA residents from Article IX,
  and no statutory inclusion either. Nothing in 210 ILCS 135, 59 IAC 115 or 59 IAC 120 resolves
  it. Illinois Legal Aid Online's CILA page does not address tenancy or eviction at all.
  **Recorded as: could not confirm.**
- Which of the two branches Illinois considers itself to be on. IDHS's own materials point both
  ways: DD.23.008 says a provider "can add resident responsibilities consistent with Illinois
  Landlord/Tenant law," implying it applies, while the residency-agreement machinery is exactly
  the fallback the federal rule prescribes for settings where it does not.

What Illinois *does* clearly supply, regardless of how that resolves, is the administrative
route in items 3-6 below. That is the operative protection in practice.

Related, and quotable on its own: DD.23.008 prohibits a provider from "Moving to evict because
the individual or guardian has not sent back and signed the residency agreement," and from
evicting for property damage arising before the initial agreement.

## 3. Notice period(s), short-notice exception, and who approves it

**Direct-fetch, tier 1 and tier 2. There is a genuine 30-day-vs-60-day discrepancy between tiers
— both are recorded, neither is resolved by guessing.**

| Source | Tier | Notice required | Category |
|---|---|---|---|
| 59 Ill. Adm. Code 115.215(b)(3) | 1 | "Include a time frame for the action" — **no number** | CILA |
| 59 Ill. Adm. Code 120.70(h)(3)(C) | 1 | "the provider shall issue the Individual and guardian at least a 30-day notice" | DDD Waiver services, incl. CILA |
| IDHS IB DD.23.008; IDHS Residency Agreement Template | 2 | 60 days | CILA / CLF / CGH residency agreement |
| 59 Ill. Adm. Code 115.460(a) | 1 | 60 days, to the Department, before an **agency ceases operations** | CILA — different action, see below |
| 210 ILCS 47/3-402 | 1 | 21 days | **ID/DD facility (ICF/DD) — not CILA** |

Read together: the enforceable floor in the administrative code is **30 days**; IDHS's own
residency-agreement policy sets **60 days** for terminating a residency agreement. The 60-day
figure is the one that appears in the document a resident actually signs, and it is the state's
own instruction to providers, so it is quotable — but it is tier 2, and the code says 30.

**Do not confuse 115.460 with an individual discharge.** That 60 days is a CILA *agency* closing
its doors and notifying IDHS, and it can be shortened only "with written approval from the
Department."

**Short-notice / emergency exception (CILA, waiver):** 59 Ill. Adm. Code 120.110(j)(1)-(2).
Services may end before the final administrative decision only where "The physical safety or
health of the Individual or others is in extreme risk of harm" or appropriate medical services
are unavailable at the agency, **and** the ISC agency has done five specified things, the last of
which is "Consulted with the Division of Developmental Disabilities." Even then, notice must be
given "as soon as possible, but in no case later than **48 hours** before the termination,
suspension, or reduction in services."

**Who approves.** Two distinct approvals, and they matter:
- 59 Ill. Adm. Code 115.215(c): "Termination of services shall occur only if the termination
  recommendation has been approved by the Department." A CILA provider cannot terminate on its
  own say-so at all.
- The emergency shortcut in 120.110(j)(1)(C) requires ISC-agency findings plus **consultation
  with** DDD — consultation, not approval. Weaker than the 115.215(c) gate. Worth flagging.

**Prerequisite step before any termination:** 59 Ill. Adm. Code 115.214(c) expects "at least 30
calendar days" to be invested in trying additional staffing, consultation and plan revision
before termination is even recommended. That is a pre-notice period, on top of the notice period.

## 4. Is there an appeal, and is it ADMINISTRATIVE or JUDICIAL?

**ADMINISTRATIVE, two steps, with judicial review only at the end. Direct-fetch, tier 1.**

This is the key comparison point and Illinois lands on the Minnesota side, not the California
side. There is no requirement that the resident file anything in court to keep their home.

Route for a DDD Waiver participant (59 Ill. Adm. Code 120.110):

1. **Trigger the appeal through the ISC.** 120.110(b): the desire to appeal "shall be
   communicated to the ISC within **10 working days** after the date the Individual or guardian
   receives the notice of action from the provider," followed by a signed written request.
   *This is a short and easily missed deadline — arguably the weakest link in the Illinois
   scheme.*
2. **DDD informal review.** 120.110(d): within 30 working days of receiving the appeal packet,
   DDD conducts an informal review and upholds or does not uphold the action; written decision
   within 10 working days after.
3. **HFS administrative hearing.** If DDD does not support the appeal, DDD forwards it to
   Healthcare and Family Services, the single state Medicaid agency, within 10 working days.
   120.110(f): "The hearing shall be conducted by an impartial hearing officer appointed by
   HFS." Hearing rules at 89 Ill. Adm. Code 104. The HFS Director issues the final
   administrative decision.
4. **Judicial review is downstream only.** For non-waiver individuals, 115.250(c) routes the CILA
   grievance decision to review "in accordance with the Administrative Review Law
   [735 ILCS 5/Art. III]" — i.e., circuit court review of an agency record, which is a different
   and much narrower thing than defending an eviction.

Distinguish the three things the scope document warns about:
- **Internal provider grievance** — 59 Ill. Adm. Code 115.250(c), grievances "up to and including
  the authorized CILA agency representative." Must be described in the notice, but it is not the
  appeal that stops a discharge.
- **Administrative fair hearing** — 120.110, above. This is the real remedy.
- **Court** — only as Administrative Review Law review after a final agency decision, or (for the
  ICF/DD category) an eviction/tenancy posture. Not the front-line remedy in a CILA.

Also worth knowing: **the provider has no appeal.** 59 Ill. Adm. Code 120.70(i): "Providers may
not appeal the Department's decisions related to discharge, termination, or reduction of services
to an Individual."

## 5. Must the NOTICE ITSELF state the appeal right?

**YES. This is the strongest finding for Illinois. Direct-fetch, tier 1, in two separate rules
plus a tier-2 bulletin.**

- **59 Ill. Adm. Code 115.215(b)(6)** — a notice of termination issued by a CILA provider must
  "Include a complete statement of the Individual's right to appeal, including the provider's
  grievance process."
- **59 Ill. Adm. Code 120.70(h)(3)(F)** goes further and names the forums: "Include a complete
  statement of the Individual's right to appeal, including the provider's grievance process; it
  must also include the Department's informal review process and HFS' hearing process as
  described in Section 120.110."
- **IDHS IB DD.23.008** states the prohibition from the other direction: the provider is
  prohibited from "Requiring an individual to move out without providing written notice
  explaining why and the right to appeal."

Illinois therefore does the thing the scope document identifies as the single strongest ask, and
does it more specifically than Minnesota in one respect: 120.70(h)(3)(F) requires the notice to
name **both** stages (DDD informal review and the HFS hearing), not just the fact of an appeal.

For the separate ICF/DD category, 210 ILCS 47/3-403(c) goes further still and dictates the exact
words and typeface — see the source library.

## 6. Is there a stay / "services continue pending appeal"?

**YES, and it is automatic on filing. No motion, no showing, no discretion. Direct-fetch, tier 1.**

**59 Ill. Adm. Code 120.110(j)**: "The receipt of the appeal shall stay the decision pending the
final administrative decision or the withdrawal of the appeal." Subsection (j)(3): "The provider
shall continue to provide services until the appeal is resolved except as described in subsection
(j)(l)."

This is stronger than Minnesota's mechanism as described in the pilot. Minn. Stat. § 245D.10
subd. 3a(d)(iv) gives the person "the right to seek a temporary order staying the termination" —
something the person must ask for. Illinois stays it by operation of the rule the moment the
appeal is received.

The only exception is the emergency route in 120.110(j)(1), summarized in item 3 above.

**Housing, not just services.** IB DD.23.008 closes the gap the scope document flags between
"ending the service" and "ending the tenancy": "The individual will be required to move unless
they successfully appeal the termination of the Residency Agreement and resulting discharge. The
provider must maintain the individual's placement pending the outcome of the appeal process."
Even in the danger-to-self-or-others scenario, the bulletin says "The provider must maintain the
individual's placement pending the identification of an alternative waiver setting."

That last sentence is the answer to "a person can lose a home either way." Illinois ties the
placement to the appeal, not only the service.

## Category-boundary note (the trap, handled)

Illinois has at least four distinct provider-housing categories for adults with DD, and their
discharge rules are **not** the same. Recorded explicitly so no one imports one into another:

| Category | Governing law | Discharge notice | Appeal forum | Stay |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **CILA** (most DD adults) | 210 ILCS 135; 59 IAC 115; 59 IAC 120 | 30 days by rule / 60 days by IDHS policy; 48 hours in emergency | DDD informal review → HFS administrative hearing | Automatic on receipt of appeal, 120.110(j) |
| **CLF (≤16 beds) and CGH** | Same residency-agreement bulletin DD.23.008; licensing act differs | 60 days per DD.23.008 | Same waiver appeal if a waiver participant | Same, per DD.23.008 |
| **ID/DD facility (ICF/DD)** | 210 ILCS 47 | 21 days, 210 ILCS 47/3-402, with two emergency exceptions | **IDPH** hearing, request within 10 days, held within 10 days, decided within 14 | Yes — 210 ILCS 47/3-404, a hearing request "shall stay a transfer" |
| Individually leased/own home | Ordinary Illinois landlord/tenant law | n/a | Court | n/a |

**Confirmed exclusion, direct-fetch:** 210 ILCS 47/1-113 provides that "Facility" does not include
"(7) Any facility licensed by the Department of Human Services as a community-integrated living
arrangement as defined in the Community-Integrated Living Arrangements Licensure and
Certification Act". So the 21-day/IDPH-hearing regime **does not apply to a CILA**, and the
CILA regime does not apply to an ICF/DD. Any answer must name the category first.

### Unresolved conflicts and cautions

1. **30 days vs 60 days.** 59 Ill. Adm. Code 120.70(h)(3)(C) sets "at least a 30-day notice."
   IDHS IB DD.23.008 and the IDHS residency-agreement template both say 60 days. Both are
   authentic Illinois sources; the code is tier 1 and the bulletin is tier 2. The most defensible
   framing is: the rule floor is 30 days, and IDHS policy for terminating a *residency agreement*
   in a provider-owned setting is 60 days. **Do not state a single number without saying which
   instrument it comes from.** Rule 115 itself supplies no number at all.
2. **The template's stay citation is wrong.** The IDHS template tells residents they have "the
   right to seek a temporary order staying the termination of services according to the procedures
   in Rule 115." Rule 115 contains no stay procedure. The Illinois stay is at 59 Ill. Adm. Code
   120.110(j), and it is *automatic on receipt of the appeal* — better than what the template
   describes, not worse. The template's phrasing tracks Minn. Stat. § 245D.10 subd. 3a(d)(iv)
   almost word for word, which suggests it was adapted from another state's language. Cite
   120.110(j), not the template, when arguing the stay.
3. **Internal cross-reference error in the code.** 120.70(h)(3)(C) points to "Section 120.110(i)"
   for emergency situations; 120.110(i) is actually "Appeals by providers," and the emergency
   provisions are at 120.110(j). Same error appears at 115.214(b) ("59 Ill. Adm. Code 120.110(i)").
   Quote as printed, argue from (j).
4. **DD.23.008 miscites "59 ILCS 120."** There is no such statute; the reference is to 59 Ill.
   Adm. Code 120. Quoted verbatim above with the error preserved.
5. **The 10-working-day appeal window is the practical weak point.** 120.110(b) requires the
   intent to appeal to reach the ISC within 10 working days of receiving the notice. Everything
   good about Illinois — the stay, the two-step hearing — is downstream of hitting that deadline.
   Any plain-language chunk written from this file should lead with it.
6. **"Approved by the Department" vs "consulted with the Division."** 115.215(c) requires
   Department approval before any CILA termination. The emergency route at 120.110(j)(1)(C)(v)
   requires only that the ISC agency have "Consulted with the Division of Developmental
   Disabilities." Whether the 115.215(c) approval gate still applies in an emergency termination
   is **not resolved on the face of either rule.** Flagged, not guessed.
7. **CLF and CGH were not independently researched.** They are swept into the residency-agreement
   requirement by IB DD.23.008 and routed to Rule 115/120 for discharge, which is the basis for
   the table above. Their own licensing statutes and rules were not read. If a question turns
   specifically on a Community Living Facility, treat this file as covering CILA and verify CLF
   separately.
8. No page fetched during this research contained text addressed to an AI agent or any embedded
   instruction. All fetched content was treated as data.
