# Wisconsin — residency agreement and involuntary discharge

- **State:** Wisconsin (WI)
- **Retrieved / researched:** 2026-07-30
- **Researcher:** agent (RightsBook residency pass)
- **Method:** `drafts/residency-research-scope.md`

## Facility categories, because the answer is different in each

Wisconsin does not have one rule. An adult with a developmental disability in
provider-owned housing is almost always in one of these, and the discharge
protections are **not** the same:

| Category | Size | Authority | Discharge rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Community-Based Residential Facility (**CBRF**) | 5+ residents | Wis. Admin. Code ch. DHS 83; Wis. Stat. ch. 50 | DHS 83.31 — 30 days, **state review, and the discharge is blocked while the review is pending** |
| Licensed **Adult Family Home**, 3–4 beds | 3–4 adults | Wis. Admin. Code ch. DHS 88 | DHS 88.08 — 30 days, emergency exception, **no appeal in the rule** |
| Certified **Adult Family Home**, 1–2 beds | 1–2 adults | Wis. Admin. Code ch. DHS 82 | DHS 82.08 — 30 days, emergency exception, **no appeal in the rule** |
| Family Care / IRIS **service** authorization (any setting) | n/a | Wis. Stat. § 46.287; Wis. Admin. Code ch. DHS 10 | 10-day notice, DHA fair hearing, **continuation of services on request** |

The single most important Wisconsin fact for advocacy: **the strong protection lives
in the CBRF rule (DHS 83.31), and it does not carry over to adult family homes.** Never
quote DHS 83.31 at an adult family home, and never let a DHS 88 answer stand in for a CBRF.

## The seven questions

### 1. Is a written residency/admission agreement required?

**Yes, in every category — but under different names.**

- **CBRF:** an admission agreement is mandatory and admission is conditioned on signing it.
  `Wis. Admin. Code § DHS 83.29(2)`. Its required contents include, at (h), "Reasons and
  notice requirements for involuntary discharge or transfer, including transfers within
  the CBRF." (direct-fetch)
- **3–4 bed AFH:** "An adult family home shall have a service agreement with each person
  to be admitted to the home except a person being admitted for respite care."
  `Wis. Admin. Code § DHS 88.06(2)(b)`. Required contents at 88.06(2)(c) include
  "7. Conditions for transfer or discharge and the assistance a licensee will provide in
  relocating a resident." (direct-fetch)
- **1–2 bed AFH:** the agreement for services must contain "A statement that the sponsor
  shall give at least 30 days written notice to all parties involved if the sponsor wants
  to terminate the placement, change the services or change the charges for services."
  `Wis. Admin. Code § DHS 82.06(3)(e)6.` (direct-fetch)

**Trap check (the pilot's trap #4):** in no category does Wisconsin require the agreement
to describe an *appeal*. CBRF agreements must state the *reasons and notice requirements*
for involuntary discharge; AFH agreements must state the *conditions for transfer or
discharge*. Those are not the same sentence as "appeal process," and the difference matters.

### 2. Does state landlord/tenant law apply, or is there a carve-out?

**Effectively carved out. Wisconsin does not rely on landlord/tenant law for these
settings; it relies on the licensing rules.** This is the hinge of 42 CFR
441.301(c)(4)(vi)(A), and Wisconsin answered it on the second branch — the "for settings
in which landlord tenant laws do not apply" branch.

- `Wis. Stat. ch. 704` (Landlord and Tenant) contains **no express exclusion** for CBRFs
  or adult family homes; its definitions at § 704.01 are silent on care settings. So a
  ch. 704 tenancy is not textually impossible — but nothing in Wisconsin law affirmatively
  extends it to these settings either. (direct-fetch; **absence finding**)
- `Wis. Admin. Code § ATCP 134.01(1)` — the residential rental practices rule that
  supplies most of Wisconsin's practical tenant protections — **expressly does not apply**
  to "A dwelling unit operated by a public or private institution if occupancy is
  incidental to detention or the provision of medical, geriatric, educational, counseling,
  religious or similar services." (direct-fetch)
- Wisconsin's **Statewide Transition Plan** answers the (vi)(A) row for CBRFs by citing
  DHS 83.29 and DHS 83.31, and for 3–4 bed AFHs by quoting DHS 88.06 and DHS 88.08 in
  full, marking both **"Compliant."** It never cites ch. 704 or ATCP 134 anywhere in the
  document. The state's own position is therefore that the *licensing agreement plus the
  licensing discharge rule* is the comparable protection. (direct-fetch, tier 3, used as a
  finding aid only)

**Practical consequence:** a Wisconsin provider ending a placement is generally **not**
filing an eviction action in circuit court under ch. 799. The action is an administrative
discharge under the licensing rule. That is the opposite of California's posture and it is
why question 4 below matters so much.

### 3. Notice period(s), and short-notice exceptions

**CBRF — 30 days, and no short-notice exception in the rule.**
"Before a CBRF involuntarily discharges a resident, the licensee shall give the resident
or legal representative a 30 day written advance notice." `DHS 83.31(4)(a)1.` The CBRF
must also "provide assistance in relocating the resident and shall ensure that a living
arrangement suitable to meet the needs of the resident is available before discharging the
resident." `DHS 83.31(4)(a)2.`
Involuntary discharge is limited to seven listed reasons, `DHS 83.31(4)(b)1.–7.`, including
"imminent risk of serious harm" — but even that reason gets the 30-day notice; the rule
provides **no faster track**. An emergency medical transfer under `DHS 83.31(2)` is
explicitly *not* a discharge: the CBRF "may not involuntarily discharge the resident
unless the requirements under sub. (4) are met." (direct-fetch; **absence finding** on a
short-notice exception)
Separately, the *Department* — not the provider — may remove a resident from a licensed
facility in an emergency under `Wis. Stat. § 50.03(5m)(a)6.` That is a state protective
action, not a provider discharge. (direct-fetch)

**3–4 bed AFH — 30 days, with an emergency exception and no named approver.**
"A licensee may terminate a resident's placement only after giving the resident, the
resident's guardian, if any, the resident's service coordinator, the placing agency, if
any, and the designated representative, if any, 30 days written notice. … The 30 day
notice is not required for an emergency termination necessary to prevent harm to the
resident or other household members." `DHS 88.08` (direct-fetch)
**Who approves the emergency exception: no one.** The rule names no agency sign-off, no
prior approval, and no post-hoc review. This is the sharpest contrast with California,
where a 3-day notice requires advance Community Care Licensing approval. (**absence finding**)

**1–2 bed AFH — 30 days, same emergency exception, same absence of an approver.**
`DHS 82.08` (direct-fetch)

**Family Care / IRIS service termination — 10 days.**
"Clients shall be given written notice of any intended adverse benefit determination at
least 10 days prior to the date of the intended adverse benefit determination."
`Wis. Admin. Code § DHS 10.52(3)` (direct-fetch)

### 4. Is there an appeal, and is it ADMINISTRATIVE or JUDICIAL?

**CBRF: yes, and it is ADMINISTRATIVE.** Not a court defense, not an internal provider
grievance — a review by the Department of Health Services regional office (DQA, Bureau of
Assisted Living). The resident has 10 days from receipt of the notice to request it in
writing; the CBRF gets 7 days to submit additional justification; the Department decides
within 10 days and notifies both sides in writing. `DHS 83.31(4)(d)1.–3.` Coercion and
retaliation for requesting the review are prohibited, including retaliation against a
staff member who helps. `DHS 83.31(4)(e)` (direct-fetch)

**3–4 bed and 1–2 bed AFH: no discharge appeal found.** DHS 88.08 and DHS 82.08 create no
review of a termination of placement by anyone. What exists instead is a **rights-violation
grievance**, a different animal: `DHS 88.10(5)` routes grievances into `ch. DHS 94` (the
s. 51.61 client-rights system), or to the county procedure under `DHS 94.29` if the person
is county-placed. That system is administrative and has real rungs — program-level review
(`DHS 94.41`), administrative review by county or state (`DHS 94.42`), state-level review
(`DHS 94.43`), final state review (`DHS 94.44`) — but it is a grievance that a *right* was
violated, not an appeal of the discharge decision, and it does not on its face stop a
30-day termination clock. Do not describe it as a discharge appeal. (direct-fetch;
**absence finding** on a discharge appeal)

**Family Care / IRIS: yes, ADMINISTRATIVE, and it is the strongest route for ending
SERVICES rather than housing.** An enrollee may request a hearing under `Wis. Stat.
§ 227.44` before the **Division of Hearings and Appeals** to contest a "Reduction,
suspension, or termination of a previously authorized service."
`Wis. Stat. § 46.287(2)(a)1m.d.` Also appealable: "Development of a plan of care that is
unacceptable to the enrollee because the plan of care requires the enrollee to live in a
place that is unacceptable to the enrollee." `§ 46.287(2)(a)1m.i.` (direct-fetch)

**Trap #3, checked both ways:** in Wisconsin, ending the *placement* (the housing) and
ending the *service authorization* run on two completely separate tracks with different
deciders. A provider closing a bed uses DHS 83.31 / 88.08 / 82.08. An MCO cutting the
residential service uses DHS 10 / § 46.287. A person can lose the home through either one,
and the AFH track is by far the weaker of the two.

### 5. Must the NOTICE ITSELF state the appeal right?

**CBRF: YES. This is Wisconsin's Minnesota-grade provision.** `DHS 83.31(4)(c)` requires
every involuntary discharge notice to include, in writing:

1. the reason and justification for discharge,
2. **a statement that the resident or legal representative may ask the Department to
   review the discharge within 10 days**, and that the request must explain why the
   discharge should not take place,
3. the name, address and telephone number of the Department's regional office **director**,
4. the ombudsman program's regional office contact — and, specifically **"For residents
   with developmental disability or mental illness, the notice shall include the name,
   address and telephone number of the protection and advocacy agency designated under
   s. 51.62(2)(a), Stats."**

That last clause is a DD-specific requirement written into the notice content. (direct-fetch)

**3–4 bed and 1–2 bed AFH: NO.** DHS 88.08 and DHS 82.08 specify a 30-day written notice
and nothing about its contents — no reason requirement, no statement of any right, no
ombudsman or P&A contact. (**absence finding**, direct-fetch)

**Family Care / IRIS: YES.** The notice of an intended adverse benefit determination must
explain "The applicant's or enrollee's right to file an appeal with the CMO or request a
fair hearing" and "How to file an appeal or a fair hearing and the timelines for doing so,"
plus "the circumstances in which the enrollee's services will be continued under s. DHS
10.56 pending the outcome of an appeal, how the enrollee can request that the services be
continued," and "The availability of independent advocacy services."
`DHS 10.52(3)(b)4., 5., 7., 8.` (direct-fetch)

### 6. Is there a stay / services-continue-pending-appeal mechanism?

**CBRF: YES, and it is automatic on a timely request — no separate motion needed.**
"If a timely request is sent to the department, the CBRF may not proceed with an
involuntary discharge until the department has completed its review and notified the
resident or the resident's legal representative and the CBRF of the department's
decision." `DHS 83.31(4)(d)1.` This is stronger in form than Minnesota's, where the person
must *seek* a temporary order staying termination; in a Wisconsin CBRF the stay attaches to
the timely request itself. (direct-fetch)

**3–4 bed and 1–2 bed AFH: NO stay found.** Nothing in DHS 88.08 or DHS 82.08 pauses a
termination. The nearest thing is discretionary and narrow: `DHS 94.50(1)` lets a client
rights specialist or administrative reviewer direct a program manager "to modify the
services being provided to the client to the extent necessary to protect the client"
pending resolution of a grievance. That is interim relief over *services*, at a reviewer's
discretion, not a hold on a discharge. (direct-fetch; **absence finding**)

**Family Care / IRIS: YES, on request.** "Except as provided in sub. (2m), a CMO may not
reduce, suspend or terminate services under dispute pending the outcome of the enrollee's
appeal under s. DHS 10.53(2) or fair hearing under s. DHS 10.55 if a request for continued
benefits was made under sub. (1) or (1m)." `DHS 10.56(2)`. Two caveats worth stating
plainly to a family: the request must be made **before the effective date**
(`DHS 10.56(1), (1m)(c)-(d)`), and "The enrollee shall be liable for the cost of services
provided during the period in which services have been continued under this section if the
outcome of the appeal or fair hearing is unfavorable to the enrollee," subject to a
hardship waiver. `DHS 10.56(3)` (direct-fetch)

### Unresolved conflicts and cautions

1. **The STP paraphrases DHS 83.29 in words that are not in DHS 83.29.** The transition
   plan's CBRF (vi)(A) row presents as a quotation: "Each resident shall have a written
   agreement that is signed by and provided to each party. The agreement includes a
   description of the space to be provided to the resident, the agreed upon rate, a
   statement of the resident's rights and the terms of termination, including timeframes.
   The agreement is updated annually." — attributed to "Wis. Admin. Code § DHS 83.29."
   **That sentence is not in DHS 83.29.** The code text requires a signed, dated admission
   agreement with the contents at 83.29(2)(a)–(h); it says nothing about a description of
   the space or an annual update. This is exactly the pilot's trap #4, occurring inside a
   state document. Quote DHS 83.29 itself; do not repeat the STP's version.
2. **Is a CBRF or AFH resident a "tenant" under ch. 704?** Genuinely unresolved. Ch. 704
   has no carve-out, ATCP 134 does, and the state's own compliance narrative relies on
   neither. No Wisconsin case was located resolving whether a CBRF resident may bring or
   defend a ch. 799 eviction action. Recorded as an open question, not answered either way.
3. **Do not import DHS 83.31 into an adult family home answer.** DHS 83 governs CBRFs
   (5+ residents). DHS 88 (3–4 beds) and DHS 82 (1–2 beds) have no equivalent to the
   department review, the notice-content rule, or the stay. A large share of Wisconsin
   adults with DD live in the 1–4 bed homes, which is where the protection is thinnest.
4. **ICF/IID and nursing-home discharge rules were not researched** and are not covered
   here. Those are institutional settings under other chapters (and are outside the HCBS
   settings rule); a separate pass would be needed.
5. **The DHS 94 grievance system is not a discharge appeal.** It reviews whether a *right*
   was violated. It is available to AFH residents and it is administrative with multiple
   levels, but nothing found makes it a vehicle for contesting a termination of placement,
   and `DHS 94.50` interim relief is discretionary and directed at services, not housing.
6. **Web content handling.** No page encountered during this research contained text
   addressed to an AI agent or any instruction-like content. All sources were treated as
   data.
