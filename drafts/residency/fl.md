# Florida — residency agreement and involuntary discharge

- **State:** Florida (FL)
- **Retrieval date:** 2026-07-30
- **Researcher:** agent (RightsBook residency/discharge pass)
- **Method:** `drafts/residency-research-scope.md`
- **Headline:** Florida is a **weak** state on this topic. There is a real, quotable
  **30-day notice** rule before a provider may end services — Rule 65G-3.005(1)(a)1, F.A.C. —
  and that is essentially all a resident of an APD group home gets. There is **no state-law
  requirement of a written residency agreement** for APD-licensed DD housing, **no
  administrative appeal against the provider's decision**, **no requirement that the notice
  state any appeal right**, and **no stay**. The only administrative hearing Florida guarantees
  runs against the *Agency*, not against the provider.

## Setting categories — read this before using any answer below

Florida splits the answer by licensing category and by *who licenses it*. APD licenses the DD
residential facilities under Chapter 65G-2, F.A.C.; AHCA licenses assisted living facilities
under Chapter 429, F.S., and nursing homes under Chapter 400, Part II. The protections are very
different, and the strong ones are **not** in the DD chapter.

| Category | Licensing authority | Written agreement required? | Notice before involuntary move-out |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Foster care facility** (≤3 residents) | APD — 65G-2.0071, F.A.C. | **No** requirement found | **30 days**, Rule 65G-3.005(1)(a)1 (service termination) |
| **Group home facility** (4–15 residents) | APD — 65G-2.0072, F.A.C. | **No** requirement found | **30 days**, Rule 65G-3.005(1)(a)1 |
| **Residential habilitation center** (≥9 residents) | APD — 65G-2.0073, F.A.C. | **No** requirement found | **30 days**, Rule 65G-3.005(1)(a)1 |
| **Comprehensive transitional education program (CTEP)** | APD — 65G-2, F.A.C. | not researched | 65G-3.005 applies to APD service providers generally |
| **Supported living** (person's own home) | APD — 65G-5, F.A.C. | **Yes — a real lease or mortgage in the person's own name**, 65G-5.004(2)(a); the provider may not be the landlord, (2)(b) | Ordinary Chapter 83, Part II eviction; plus 30 days for the *service*, 65G-3.005 |
| **Assisted living facility** (many APD clients live in these) | AHCA — Ch. 429, Part I, F.S. | **Yes — a contract at or before admission**, s. 429.24(1), F.S. | **45 days**, s. 429.28(1)(k), F.S.; shorter only on a **court** showing of good cause |
| **ICF/IID** | AHCA — Ch. 400, Part VIII, F.S. | not researched in this pass | not researched in this pass |

Nothing in the strong ALF row transfers to an APD group home. Do not import it.

## 1. Is a written residency/admission agreement required for provider-owned DD housing?

**No — not by any Florida DD licensing rule. Absence finding.**

I read the operative APD licensing rules directly. The word "agreement" does not appear in
65G-2.007 (General Residential Facility Standards), 65G-2.0072 (Group Home Facility Standards),
or anywhere in 65G-2.009 (Resident Care and Supervision Standards) except one unrelated mention
of an Agency *settlement* agreement about video monitoring. There is no residency agreement,
admission agreement, resident contract, or lease requirement running to the resident.

The closest thing is an **internal-policy** requirement, not a resident-facing document:

> "(3) Transfer and Placement of Residents.(a) The licensee shall have written criteria and
> procedures in place for the admission or termination of residential services for clients;
> termination procedures must be consistent with Chapter 65G-3, F.A.C."
> — Rule 65G-2.009(3)(a), F.A.C.

That obliges the *provider* to have written criteria. It does not oblige the provider to sign
anything with the resident, does not specify contents, and does not have to be given to the
resident.

**Where the requirement actually comes from.** Florida meets 42 CFR 441.301(c)(4)(vi)(A) by
adopting the federal rule wholesale rather than writing its own:

> "59G-13.075 Home and Community Based Services Settings. (1) This rule applies to all settings
> where Florida Medicaid waiver services are rendered to individuals enrolled in home and
> community-based service (HCBS) waiver programs authorized under Sections 1915(c), 1915(i), and
> 1915(k) of the Social Security Act. (2) All settings must be in compliance with the provisions
> of the home and community-based settings requirements established in accordance with the
> Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services' (CMS) Final Rule CMS-2296-F (HCB Settings Rule)
> March 17, 2014, incorporated by reference…"
> — Rule 59G-13.075(1)–(2), F.A.C.

So the legally enforceable written agreement is required in Florida **only** as a condition of
Medicaid payment, by reference to the federal text, and enforced through AHCA's setting
assessment and a plan of remediation (59G-13.075(4)–(5)). It is not in the resident's licensing
chapter and it is not a right the resident can invoke against the provider.

Florida's own Statewide Transition Plan confirms this by scoring the (vi)(A) row
**"Compliant/Silent"** and pointing at the ALF rules and the managed-care contract rather than
at any DD rule — see the quote at §2.

## 2. Does state landlord/tenant law apply, or is there a carve-out?

**There is an express statutory carve-out, and it very likely swallows APD group homes.**

> "83.42 Exclusions from application of part. — This part does not apply to: (1) Residency or
> detention in a facility, whether public or private, when residence or detention is incidental
> to the provision of medical, geriatric, educational, counseling, religious, or similar
> services. For residents of a facility licensed under part II of chapter 400, the provisions of
> s. 400.0255 are the exclusive procedures for all transfers and discharges."
> — § 83.42(1), Fla. Stat. (2025)

Two things to say carefully:

- The exclusion is **fact-dependent**, not categorical. It turns on whether residence is
  "incidental to the provision of medical… or similar services." An APD group home exists to
  deliver residential habilitation, which is exactly that posture. I did **not** find Florida
  appellate case law applying § 83.42(1) to a Chapter 393 facility, and I did not search case
  law systematically — treat "the carve-out applies" as strongly indicated, not settled.
- The Legislature knew how to substitute a replacement procedure when it excluded a category:
  it did so for nursing homes (§ 400.0255). It wrote **no** equivalent replacement for Chapter
  393 facilities. That is the gap.

Someone raised precisely this with the State during the transition-plan comment period, and the
State declined to change anything:

> "The State received comments about 42 CFR 441.530(a)(1)(vi)(A) and asserted Florida is not
> compliant with rule requirements. The commenter claims there is a gap between protections
> afforded tenants under Chapter 83, Florida Statutes and HCBS beneficiaries and provided
> suggestions on remedying the gap. The State reviewed and considered the comments received.
> Florida has implemented the HCB settings Final Rule and developed the Statewide Transition Plan
> in accordance with CFR 441.530(a)(1)(vi)(A), including the promulgation of Rule 59G-13.075,
> Home and Community Based Services Settings, effective 12/25/18, that incorporates the federal
> HCB settings language by reference…"
> — Florida Statewide Transition Plan (October 2022), Summary of Public Comments (comment period
> March 25 – April 24, 2022)

The same plan's crosswalk records, for the (vi)(A) standard, that Florida relies on ALF rules
and the Statewide Medicaid Managed Care contract, and notes the residual silence:

> "Rule 59A-36.018(1), F.A.C. is silent related to the residents' responsibilities and
> protections from eviction. The State promulgated Rule 59G-13.075, F.A.C. in 2018, making it
> fully compliant with this regulation."
> — Florida Statewide Transition Plan (October 2022), systemic assessment crosswalk,
> 42 CFR 441.301(c)(4)(vi)(A) row

**Contrast — supported living.** If the person is in supported living rather than a licensed
facility, Chapter 83 applies normally, because the rule forces a genuine tenancy:

> "(2) The following criteria identify an eligible supported living setting:(a) The name of the
> individual appears on the lease or mortgage either singularly, with a roommate, or with a
> guarantor;(b) Neither the supported living provider nor the immediate family of the supported
> living provider shall serve as landlord or have any interest in the ownership of the housing
> unit"
> — Rule 65G-5.004(2), F.A.C.

That is the one Florida setting where the resident holds real eviction defenses — and it is the
one setting that by definition is **not** provider-owned.

## 3. Notice period(s) before involuntary discharge, and any short-notice exception

**30 calendar days, running from receipt, not from mailing.**

> "(a) If a provider determines that he or she must terminate or reduce services the provider
> gives to a client, the provider shall send written notice of intent to terminate or reduce
> services to the client and their individual representative by certified mail or electronic
> mail. The provider shall send the required notice to the Regional Office and Support
> Coordinator via electronic mail.
> 1. The provider may not terminate or reduce services until at least thirty calendar days after
> the receipt of the notification by the client, individual representative, Regional Office, and
> Support Coordinator."
> — Rule 65G-3.005(1)(a), F.A.C.

Note the clock: 30 days after **all four** recipients have received it.

**Short-notice exception: none found for a residential setting.** This is a real finding and
worth stating out loud. Rule 65G-3.005(2) does create emergency and behavior-based
**suspensions**, but both are expressly limited to non-residential programs:

> "(a) Emergency Suspension: A service provider may suspend services provided to a client in a
> non-residential program when the behavior of the client constitutes an immediate danger to
> health, safety, or welfare of the client, another recipient of the provider's services, the
> provider, or a third party."
> — Rule 65G-3.005(2)(a), F.A.C.

> "(b) Other Suspension: A service provider may suspend services provided to a client in a
> non-residential program when the client's behavior interferes with services provided to other
> recipients of the provider's services… 1. This type of suspension shall not exceed 3 service
> days."
> — Rule 65G-3.005(2)(b), F.A.C.

So on the face of the rule a residential provider has no lawful emergency route to end services
faster than 30 days. There is an emergency provision on the *transfer-approval* side, but it
waives APD's approval, not the resident's notice:

> "(h) A licensee who operates, administers, or manages more than one foster care facility, group
> home facility or residential habilitation center facility must receive approval from the Agency
> prior to transferring a client from one of its licensed facilities to another of its licensed
> facilities. Prior approval shall not be required in the event of an emergency…"
> — Rule 65G-2.009(3)(h), F.A.C.

**Who else must act in the 30 days.** The Support Coordinator must evaluate the circumstances,
try to resolve them, and immediately help find an alternate provider (65G-3.005(1)(b)); on
request must convene a meeting with the provider within five calendar days (65G-3.005(1)(c));
must escalate to the APD Regional Office at least 25 days before the effective date if help is
needed (65G-3.005(1)(d)); and for a licensed residential facility:

> "(e) If the termination is for services provided in a licensed residential facility, the
> Regional Office shall immediately work with the client's Support Coordinator to assist the
> client or the client's individual representative in choosing an alternate place to live before
> the date of termination."
> — Rule 65G-3.005(1)(e), F.A.C.

That is a **relocation-assistance** duty, not a right to stay.

**Category contrast (ALF only).** If the setting is an ALF licensed under Chapter 429, the
resident gets 45 days and the short-notice route runs through a judge:

> "(k) At least 45 days' notice of relocation or termination of residency from the facility
> unless, for medical reasons, the resident is certified by a physician to require an emergency
> relocation to a facility providing a more skilled level of care or the resident engages in a
> pattern of conduct that is harmful or offensive to other residents… Reasons for relocation must
> be set forth in writing and provided to the resident or the resident's legal representative.
> The notice must state that the resident may contact the State Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program
> for assistance with relocation and must include the statewide toll-free telephone number of the
> program. In order for a facility to terminate the residency of an individual without notice as
> provided herein, the facility shall show good cause in a court of competent jurisdiction."
> — § 429.28(1)(k), Fla. Stat. (2025)

## 4. Is there an appeal, and is it ADMINISTRATIVE or JUDICIAL?

**Against the provider's decision to discharge: neither. There is no appeal.** Rule 65G-3.005
gives the resident a *negotiation*, not a forum — a support-coordinator-convened meeting within
five days on request (65G-3.005(1)(c)), whose only output is "recommendations." Nothing in
Chapter 65G-3 or Chapter 65G-2 lets a resident contest the provider's termination before any
tribunal.

**Against the Agency's decision: yes, ADMINISTRATIVE.** Florida's DD hearing right runs only to
agency action:

> "393.125 Hearing rights.—(1) REVIEW OF AGENCY DECISIONS.—(a) For Medicaid programs administered
> by the agency, any developmental services applicant or client, or his or her parent, guardian
> advocate, or authorized representative, may request a hearing in accordance with federal law
> and rules applicable to Medicaid cases and has the right to request an administrative hearing
> pursuant to ss. 120.569 and 120.57. These hearings shall be provided by the Department of
> Children and Families pursuant to s. 409.285 and shall follow procedures consistent with
> federal law and rules applicable to Medicaid cases. (b) Any other developmental services
> applicant or client… who has any substantial interest determined by the agency, has the right
> to request an administrative hearing pursuant to ss. 120.569 and 120.57…"
> — § 393.125(1)(a)–(b), Fla. Stat. (2025)

The forum is the DCF **Office of Appeal Hearings** (Medicaid fair hearing under s. 409.285,
Fla. Stat., procedures in Chapter 65-2, F.A.C.) or a Chapter 120 hearing. The request deadline
is 30 days from receipt of the agency's notice — § 393.125(1)(d).

The Legislature did contemplate provider-side due process, but only as a rulemaking directive,
and the rule APD wrote in response (65G-3.005) contains no hearing:

> "(2) REVIEW OF PROVIDER DECISIONS.—The agency shall adopt rules to establish uniform guidelines
> for the agency and service providers relevant to termination, suspension, or reduction of
> client services by the service provider. The rules shall ensure the due process rights of
> service providers and clients."
> — § 393.125(2), Fla. Stat. (2025)

**Trap check — whose right is it?** Chapter 65G-5 contains rules titled "Suspension of Provider
Certification" (65G-5.010) and "Termination of Provider Certification" (65G-5.011). Those are
APD actions **against the provider**. They are not resident appeal rights. Do not cite them.

**Ending services vs. eviction — three separate tracks in Florida:**
1. *Ending the service* (residential habilitation) by the provider → 65G-3.005, 30 days, no
   appeal against the provider.
2. *Agency* reduction/denial/termination of waiver services → § 393.125 administrative fair
   hearing, DCF Office of Appeal Hearings.
3. *Physical removal from the dwelling* → Chapter 83, Part II eviction **if** the setting is not
   caught by the § 83.42(1) exclusion. In an APD group home it likely is caught, leaving no
   defined judicial procedure at all. This is the sharpest gap in Florida.

## 5. Must the NOTICE ITSELF state the appeal right?

**Not the provider's discharge notice. No.** Rule 65G-3.005(1)(a) prescribes the delivery method
(certified or electronic mail) and the recipients. It prescribes **no content at all** — not the
reason, not a right, not a contact.

This is exactly the ambiguity the scope warns about, and Florida contains the trap in one rule.
Rule 65G-3.005 *does* require an explanation of hearing rights — in a different notice, issued by
a different party, about a different decision:

> "(f) If the client or the client's individual representative selects an individual or entity to
> render the services who is not qualified to be that client's provider, the Regional Office
> shall issue a notice to deny the client's selection of provider choice within 30 calendar days
> of the client's notification to the Regional Office. The notice shall identify: 1. Any alternate
> providers or service options for the client that the Agency has determined are readily
> available; 2. The reason the Agency is denying the client's selection of provider choice; 3. The
> specific statute or rule supporting the denial; and 4. An explanation of the client's
> administrative hearing rights pursuant to sections 393.125, 120.569, and 120.57, F.S."
> — Rule 65G-3.005(1)(f), F.A.C.

That is APD's notice refusing the *replacement* provider the person picked after being
discharged. It is not the discharge notice.

**Where Florida does require it — agency notices only, and it is a strong requirement:**

> "(c) Notice of the right to an administrative hearing shall be given, both verbally and in
> writing, to the applicant or client, and his or her parent, guardian, guardian advocate, or
> authorized representative, at the same time that the agency gives the applicant or client notice
> of the agency's action. The notice shall be given, both verbally and in writing, in the language
> of the client or applicant and in English."
> — § 393.125(1)(c), Fla. Stat. (2025)

Verbally **and** in writing, in the person's own language **and** English, simultaneously with the
action notice. That is a better notice standard than most states have — and it is bolted to the
wrong actor for a discharge. Florida's gap is not that it lacks the concept; it is that the
concept was never extended to the provider's notice.

## 6. Is there a stay / "services continue pending appeal"?

**Could not confirm any state-law stay. Absence finding.**

- Chapter 65G-3, F.A.C. — no stay. The 30 days run and the service ends.
- Chapter 65G-2, F.A.C. — no stay.
- § 393.125, Fla. Stat. — creates the hearing right and a 30-day filing window; says nothing
  about benefits continuing.
- Rule 65-2.049, F.A.C. (DCF hearing request procedures) — read in full; contains a supervisory
  review and withdrawal mechanism only, **no continuation-of-benefits provision**.

Because a provider-initiated discharge is not agency action, there is nothing for a stay to
attach to in the first place. Where the *Agency* reduces or terminates waiver services, federal
Medicaid continuation-of-benefits ("aid paid pending", 42 CFR 431.230) is the likely mechanism
and is referenced generally by Florida practitioners, but **I did not fetch or verify the federal
rule or any Florida implementation of it in this pass** — treat it as unverified and check
42 CFR 431.230 plus Chapter 65-2, F.A.C. before relying on it.

## 7. Bottom line for the corpus chunk

Florida gives a person in an APD group home one hard number and very little else: **30 days'
written notice, counted from receipt, before the provider may end residential services**
(Rule 65G-3.005(1)(a)1, F.A.C.). During those 30 days the Support Coordinator and the APD
Regional Office must help find somewhere else to live. There is **no appeal of the provider's
decision**, the notice does **not** have to mention any rights, and there is **no way to pause the
move**. The administrative fair hearing Florida does provide (§ 393.125) is aimed at APD's own
decisions, and when APD sends *that* kind of notice the law is emphatic — it must state the
hearing right verbally and in writing, in the person's language and English, at the same time.
Ordinary eviction protections are probably unavailable, because § 83.42(1) excludes residency
that is "incidental to the provision of medical… or similar services," and unlike nursing homes
the Legislature substituted nothing in their place.

## Traps encountered, and how they were handled

1. **A tier-2 PDF that looked primary and was not.** APD publishes
   `https://apd.myflorida.com/news/news/docs/Revised_Chapter_65G-2.pdf`, a clean 51-page full text
   of Chapter 65G-2 that reads exactly like the codified rule. It is a **proposed-rule draft** — its
   history lines read "History–New ________" and its section numbering (e.g. subsection "(3)
   TRANSFER AND PLACEMENT OF CLIENTS" with paragraphs (a)–(j)) does **not** match the adopted rule
   (which reads "(3) Transfer and Placement of Residents" with paragraphs (a)–(j) renumbered and
   several paragraphs added). Nothing from that PDF is quoted here; every 65G-2 quote comes from
   the current adopted `.doc` on flrules.org. **This is precisely the scope's "search summaries
   drift from the source" trap, one level up: an official agency site hosting a non-current draft.**
2. **Whose appeal is it.** Chapter 65G-5 has rules titled "Suspension of Provider Certification"
   and "Termination of Provider Certification." Those are APD actions against providers, not
   resident remedies. Flagged in §4 so nobody cites them as a resident's appeal.
3. **Which notice must state the right.** 65G-3.005 contains an "explanation of the client's
   administrative hearing rights" requirement, and it would be easy to attach it to the discharge
   notice. It attaches to the Regional Office's *denial of the client's replacement-provider
   choice* — subsection (1)(f), a different notice by a different party. Handled explicitly in §5.
4. **Category discipline.** The strong Florida numbers (45 days, written contract, court showing
   of good cause, ombudsman phone number) are all **Chapter 429 assisted living facility** rules
   licensed by AHCA. They are recorded here as a deliberate contrast and are labeled at every
   occurrence. They do **not** apply to APD foster care facilities, group homes, or residential
   habilitation centers.

## Unresolved conflicts / open questions

- **Does § 83.42(1) actually exclude an APD group home?** Strongly indicated by the text, not
  confirmed by case law. No Florida appellate decision applying § 83.42(1) to a Chapter 393
  facility was located, and case law was not searched systematically. If a court has held the
  other way, Florida's answer to Q2 changes materially. Flag for a lawyer.
- **The iBudget handbook was not read.** Rule 59G-13.070, F.A.C. incorporates the *Developmental
  Disabilities Individual Budgeting Waiver Services Coverage and Limitations Handbook* by
  reference. The Statewide Transition Plan cites that handbook repeatedly and never once cites it
  for a residency-agreement or eviction-protection requirement — which is why the Q1 answer is
  "no requirement found" — but the handbook itself was not opened in this pass.
- **Whether the SMMC contract term is enforceable by a resident.** The STP says the Statewide
  Medicaid Managed Care contract, Attachment II, Exhibit II-B, "specifically requires residential
  agreements between individuals and facilities." That is a contract between AHCA and health
  plans. Whether an APD iBudget group-home resident (not in an SMMC plan for residential services)
  is covered by it at all was not determined.
- **ICF/IID discharge rules were not researched.** ICF/IIDs are AHCA-licensed institutional
  settings outside the HCBS settings rule, with their own Chapter 400 / 59A framework. Recorded as
  a gap rather than assumed to follow the group-home answer.
