# Pennsylvania — residency agreement and involuntary discharge

- **State:** Pennsylvania (PA)
- **Researcher:** research agent
- **Retrieval date for all sources below:** 2026-07-30
- **Primary regulatory home:** 55 Pa. Code Chapter 6100 (Services for Individuals with an
  Intellectual Disability or Autism), with licensing chapters 6400 (community homes) and
  6500 (life sharing homes).

**Facility categories covered by the answers below.** Chapter 6100 governs 1915(c) waiver
HCBS, State plan HCBS, and county base-funded ID/autism services (§ 6100.2(a)-(c)). It
**expressly does not apply** to ICF/IID (Chapter 6600), personal care homes (Chapter 2600),
assisted living residences (Chapter 2800), nursing facilities, hospitals, mental health
facilities, or privately funded placements (§ 6100.2(d)). So every notice and agreement
answer below is for **waiver/base-funded residential habilitation in provider-owned or
-leased community homes (Ch. 6400) and life sharing homes (Ch. 6500)**. ICF/IID discharge
is governed instead by the federal ICF/IID conditions of participation, which Pennsylvania
incorporates by reference at 55 Pa. Code § 6600.3 — **not researched here**, and the 45-day
rule below must not be imported into it.

---

## 1. Is a written residency/admission agreement required? Which regulation?

**Yes.** 55 Pa. Code § 6100.687 requires a **Room and Board Residency Agreement** "on a form
specified by the Department," completed and signed **annually**, and signed before admission,
before a transfer between residential locations or providers, or within 15 days after an
emergency placement (§ 6100.687(d)).

Scope is exactly the population in question: § 6100.681 applies the room and board sections
"for the room and board rate charged to the individual in provider owned or leased
residential service locations and in life sharing homes that are not owned or leased by the
individual."

The Department-specified form is **Form DP 1077 (rev. 5/24)**, issued as Attachment 4 to ODP
Bulletin 00-25-01 (March 17, 2025). The bulletin states that "Form DP 1077 must be used for
all Room and Board Agreements. Alternative agreements or addendums to Form DP 1077 are
prohibited." The form itself repeats: "The provider must use the DP 1077 and may not alter
the form." The agreement "may only be effective for a maximum of 12 months from the effective
date."

Copies must go to the individual, the designated person, and any court-appointed guardian
(§ 6100.689(a)), and be kept in the individual's record (§ 6100.689(b)).

**Verification: direct-fetch** (Pa. Code official site; pa.gov PDF).

## 2. Does state landlord/tenant law apply, or is there a carve-out?

**Could not confirm.** No Pennsylvania statute, regulation, or state agency document located
in this pass states either (a) that the Landlord and Tenant Act of 1951 (68 P.S. §§ 250.101
et seq.) applies to licensed community homes or life sharing homes, or (b) that those settings
are carved out of it.

What Pennsylvania *does* say is that the residency agreement is the vehicle for the
42 CFR 441.301(c)(4)(vi)(A) protection. ODP Bulletin 00-25-01 states: "The Room and Board
Agreement provides all individuals receiving Residential Habilitation or Life Sharing services
with the protections of a legally enforceable agreement." That framing — the agreement supplies
the protection — is the state's answer to (vi)(A), but the bulletin does not say whether
landlord/tenant law also applies, and it does not claim the agreement's protections are
"comparable to" landlord/tenant protections.

Where I looked and did not find it: 55 Pa. Code §§ 6100.1-6100.3, 6100.181-6100.186,
6100.301-6100.307, 6100.681-6100.694; 55 Pa. Code Ch. 6400 and Ch. 6500 tables of contents
(neither chapter has a discharge, eviction, tenancy, or residency-agreement section);
Pennsylvania's CMS-approved Statewide Transition Plan (87 pages, full-text searched for
"landlord," "tenant," "evict," "lease," "residency agreement," "441.301" — the STP contains
no discussion of the (vi)(A) legally-enforceable-agreement or eviction-protection criterion
at all); ODP Bulletin 00-25-01 and Form DP 1077.

This is a genuine gap in the public record, not a research failure. **Do not assert either way.**

**Verification: direct-fetch for the absence** (all four documents fetched and full-text
searched). Two corroborating documents were **blocked** — see Verification notes.

## 3. Notice period(s) before involuntary discharge, and short-notice exception

**45 days.** 55 Pa. Code § 6100.304(a): written notice at least 45 days before the proposed
change of provider or transfer, to the individual, persons the individual designates, the
individual plan team, the designated managing entity, the support coordinator, and the
Department.

**Grounds are closed-ended.** § 6100.303(a) lists "the only grounds": danger to self or
others at that location even with supplemental services; needs changed/advanced/declined
beyond what the provider can meet even with supplemental services; meeting needs would require
significant alteration of the provider's program or building; or closure of the service
location. § 6100.303(b) forbids retaliatory transfer.

**Short-notice exception and who approves it.** § 6100.304(b): "The Department or designated
managing entity may authorize a transfer or change date earlier than the date specified in
subsection (a) to protect the health and safety of the individual or others." So the approver
is DHS or the designated managing entity (in ODP practice, the Administrative Entity) — **not**
the provider acting alone. There is no floor on how short the shortened notice may be.

**A separate provider-side loophole.** Form DP 1077, Part II.3, allows the provider to
"temporarily relocate the individual to another location that is not the address of residence
due to emergency circumstances," and states: "The provider is not required to give written
notice as described in 6100.301-307." The agreement's terms continue to apply, and "temporary"
is not defined or time-limited on the form.

**Verification: direct-fetch.**

## 4. Is there an appeal, and is it ADMINISTRATIVE or JUDICIAL?

**Neither, at the level of the discharge itself. What the residency agreement gives is an
internal provider grievance.** Form DP 1077, section "COMPLAINTS": "The individual has the
right to file an oral or written complaint with the provider agency if they disagree with the
termination of this agreement," managed under 55 Pa. Code § 6100.51. That is a complaint to
the same provider that issued the discharge — not a neutral forum. § 6100.51(h) gives the
provider 30 days to resolve it, and § 6100.51(d) bars retaliation, but no independent review
of the discharge decision is created.

Chapter 6400 § 6400.23 likewise requires only that "The home shall have written grievance
procedures for individuals, individual's families, advocates and staff persons, that assure
investigation and resolution of complaints."

**The two sections labeled "Appeals" in these chapters are not the individual's appeal.**
- § 6100.41: "Appeals related to this chapter shall be made in accordance with Chapter 41
  (relating to **medical assistance provider appeal procedures**)." That is the provider's
  appeal route.
- § 6400.12: appeals "related to the Department's licensure," under the Administrative Agency
  Law — again not the resident's.

**What administrative appeal does exist, and its limit.** Pennsylvania has a general DHS fair
hearing system before the Office of Hearings and Appeals under 55 Pa. Code Chapter 275, and it
reaches services, not just cash: § 275.1(a)(4)(i)(C) gives a right of appeal from "A denial,
discontinuance, reduction or exclusion from a Departmental service program including the
failure to take into account the client's choice of a service." § 275.1(a)(3) defines
"departmental" to include "agencies which administer or provide social services under
contractual arrangements with the Department."

**But nothing located in this pass connects that hearing right to a provider's
§ 6100.303/.304 involuntary transfer.** Chapter 275 attaches to a *Departmental* action with
a written departmental notice (Forms PA 5-M, PA 162, PA 162-A, PA 162-C — § 275.1(a)(4)(i)(B)).
Chapter 6100 does not require the Department or the managing entity to issue any such notice
when a provider discharges, and § 6100.304's notice content list does not mention one.
Whether an individual can appeal an AE's approval of a shortened transfer date under
§ 6100.304(b) is **could not confirm** — no source found either way.

**Summary for the compare table: PA = internal provider grievance only, as to the discharge
itself. Not administrative. Not judicial. A general MA administrative fair hearing exists in
Pennsylvania but is not tied to this action by any located authority.**

**Verification: direct-fetch** for every regulation quoted; the "not connected" conclusion is
an absence finding across the sources listed.

## 5. Must the NOTICE ITSELF state the appeal right?

**No. This is a clean, quotable absence.**

55 Pa. Code § 6100.304(c) is an exhaustive list of what the written notice "must include":
the individual's name and master client index number; the current provider's name, address and
master provider index number; the service the provider is unable or unwilling to provide; the
location where the service is currently provided; the reason under § 6100.303; "A description
of the efforts made to address or resolve the issue"; and "Suggested time frames for
transitioning the delivery of the service to the new provider."

**Nothing in that list is a statement of any appeal, hearing, complaint, or grievance right.**
Form DP 1077 mentions the complaint right in the agreement, but the agreement is signed at
admission, up to a year before any discharge; the 45-day notice that actually ends the
placement carries no such statement.

Pennsylvania does require rights to be explained at admission and annually — § 6400.34(a)
("shall inform and explain individual rights and the process to report a rights violation to
the individual... upon admission to the home and annually thereafter") and § 6100.51(b)
(inform of the right to file a complaint "upon initial entry into the provider's program and
annually thereafter"). That is an annual conversation, not a statement on the discharge notice.

**Contrast for advocacy: Minnesota requires the termination notice itself to state the appeal
right and the right to seek a stay (Minn. Stat. § 245D.10, subd. 3a(d)). Pennsylvania's
§ 6100.304(c) requires neither.**

## 6. Is there a stay / "services continue pending appeal" mechanism?

**There is no stay pending appeal. There is something different and, in one respect, stronger:
a flat continuity-of-service duty on the provider.**

55 Pa. Code § 6100.305, in full: "The provider shall continue to provide the authorized service
during the transition period to ensure continuity of service until a new provider is approved
and the new service is in place, unless otherwise directed by the Department or the designated
managing entity."

Read plainly, the provider cannot simply stop at day 45 — it must keep serving until a new
provider is approved and in place. That is not conditioned on filing anything, which makes it
broader than a stay. But it is also **not** a stay: it does not pause or review the discharge
decision, it presumes the move will happen, and it can be switched off unilaterally by DHS or
the managing entity ("unless otherwise directed").

Separately, Pennsylvania's general fair hearing chapter does have aid-paid-pending:
55 Pa. Code § 275.4(a)(3)(v)(I) — "In cases in which a hearing is requested within the
specified time period, assistance will be continued pending the hearing decision unless the
client waives continuation of benefits." As in question 4, **no located source applies this to
a provider-initiated residential discharge**, and the § 6100.304 notice is not the kind of
departmental notice that triggers it.

**Verification: direct-fetch.**

> § 6100.303. Involuntary transfer or change of provider.
> (a) The following are the only grounds for a change in a provider or a transfer of an individual against the individual's wishes:

### Unresolved conflicts and open questions
1. **Question 2 is genuinely open.** No source says landlord/tenant law applies, and none says
   it does not. Do not fill this in from another state or from a general PA tenant guide.
2. **Whether an individual can appeal to the Office of Hearings and Appeals from a provider
   discharge — could not confirm.** Chapter 275 plainly provides a services fair hearing from
   *Departmental* action, and § 275.1(a)(3) defines "departmental" broadly enough to arguably
   reach a contracted provider. But no ODP regulation, bulletin, or notice form located here
   makes that link, and § 6100.304(c) conspicuously omits any appeal statement. Two readings
   are possible and the sources do not choose between them. A call to ODP or DRP would settle it.
3. **Two things named "appeal" in the same chapters are the provider's and the licensee's**
   (§ 6100.41, § 6400.12). Any summary that reports "Pennsylvania Chapter 6100 has an appeals
