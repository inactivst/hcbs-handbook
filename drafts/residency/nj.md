# New Jersey — residency agreement and involuntary discharge

- **State:** New Jersey (NJ)
- **Retrieval date:** 2026-07-30
- **Researcher:** agent (RightsBook residency/discharge pass)
- **Method:** `drafts/residency-research-scope.md`
- **Headline:** New Jersey is unusual. The licensing rule **requires a lease or, failing
  that, a "consumer residency agreement" that must itself carry eviction protections and
  appeal rights comparable to New Jersey landlord/tenant law** — that is a tier-1 mandate
  most states do not have. But the state does **not** by rule set a notice period, does
  **not** require the notice to state the appeal right, and the "appeal" a discharged
  resident actually gets is an **administrative** appeal against the **Division**, not
  against the provider. The Anti-Eviction Act has **no carve-out** for community
  residences — but the state's own CMS filing treats provider-managed DD settings as
  places where landlord/tenant law does not directly apply, and substitutes the residency
  agreement plus a 2005 Division Circular. That gap is the New Jersey story.

eviction statute it does so to *remove* protection, not to add it.

## 1. Is a written residency/admission agreement required for provider-owned DD housing?

**Yes — N.J.A.C. 10:44A-4.1(d) and (d)1**, in the DHS licensing chapter for community
residences. Chapter effective March 29, 2019; §4.1 rewritten by R.2023 d.021, effective
February 6, 2023.

The rule is two-tiered. A **written lease** signed by the individual, the guardian where
applicable, and the landlord is the default. Where there is no formal lease, a **"consumer
residency agreement"** must be signed instead, and — this is the strong part — that
agreement must itself supply eviction protections and appeal rights comparable to New
Jersey landlord/tenant law. Verbatim text in the Source library, quote **NJ-1**.

Note the drafting weakness: the lease requirement in (d) is qualified "**where
applicable**", and the residency agreement in (d)1 is signed by the consumer and guardian
only — **the landlord/provider is not listed as a signatory** to the residency agreement,
unlike the lease. Whether an agreement the provider never signs is enforceable against the
provider is not resolved on the face of the rule.

**What the agreement must contain** is not spelled out at rule level. The only itemised
list is in a tier-2 guidance document, the **DDD Provider Guide to the HCBS Settings
Rule**, p. 13, and it is written in "should", not "shall". It lists the provider's notice
timeframe for service termination and/or eviction, and "Information on the individual
and/or guardian's right to appeal." Quote **NJ-2**.

**Absence finding:** N.J.A.C. 10:44A-4.1(a) lists the licensee's mandatory *admission
criteria* (services provided, non-discrimination, age limits, behaviour-support techniques,
roommate choice) and says nothing about discharge conditions, notice, or appeal. The
separate mandatory disclosure packet at 10:44A-4.1(e) hands the resident a rights document,
house rules, the fee schedule, and the licensee's grievance procedure — again, no
discharge terms.

## 2. Does state landlord/tenant law apply, or is there a carve-out?

**On the face of the statute there is no carve-out. In the state's own CMS filing there
effectively is one.** This is the hinge and it is contested.

**Statute (tier 1).** N.J.S.A. 2A:18-61.1, the Anti-Eviction Act, covers "any house,
building, mobile home or land in a mobile home park or tenement leased for residential
purposes" with three exclusions: owner-occupied premises with not more than two rental
units / transient or seasonal lodging; a dwelling unit **held in trust for an immediate
family member who permanently occupies it**; and a dwelling unit **permanently occupied by
an immediate family member of the owner** — and exclusions (2) and (3) "shall apply only in
cases in which the member of the immediate family has a developmental disability." Quote
**NJ-3**.

Read that carefully, because a search summary will get it backwards. The only mention of
developmental disability in the coverage clause **removes** Anti-Eviction Act protection,
and it removes it from a family-owned or family-trust home, not from a provider-owned
group home. **A licensed community residence is not excluded by the text.** Nothing in
N.J.S.A. 30:11B (community residences for people with developmental disabilities) or in
N.J.A.C. 10:44A purports to exempt licensed DD residences from Chapter 2A:18.

The one nearby institutional carve-out is **N.J.S.A. 2A:18-59.2**, which makes the
*notice-to-quit* act inapplicable to "a residential health care facility as defined in
section 1 of P.L. 1953, c. 212 (C.30:11A-1)." That is a boarding-home category under DHS's
residential health care licensure, not a DD community residence under N.J.S.A. 30:11B. It
should not be imported into a DD answer.

**Practice (tier 3, and DHS's own words).** New Jersey's CMS Statewide Transition Plan
Addendum #3 (January 11, 2023) records a public comment arguing that DDD residency
agreements do not comply with the Anti-Eviction Act — that under N.J.S.A. 2A:18-61.1 "a
tenant can only be evicted if the landlord obtains an order for eviction and warrant for
removal from the New Jersey Superior Court – Special Civil Part." **DHS did not answer
that the Act applies.** It answered by invoking the 42 CFR 441.301(c)(4)(vi)(A) fallback
clause for "settings in which landlord tenant laws do not apply," and said that in DDD
provider-managed settings "comparable protections are provided through the residency
agreement, supported by Division Circular #36." Quote **NJ-4**.

So: no statutory carve-out, but the administering agency positions DD provider-managed
settings inside the federal *fallback*. The practical consequence is that a person's
protection depends on what the residency agreement actually says, and on a 2005 Division
Circular, rather than on the Special Civil Part. Whether a New Jersey court would agree is
an open question this research could not resolve; no controlling case was located within
the fetch budget, and none should be asserted.

**Two distinct actions, per the trap.** N.J.A.C. 10:44A-1.3 defines "**Eviction**" as "an
actual expulsion of a tenant out of the premises." That is the tenancy action. Ending the
*service* is a separate act governed by the CCP manual §12.5 and Division Circular #36. In
a provider-owned home the two collapse in practice — losing the service means losing the
home — but they are governed by different instruments and different forums.

## 3. Notice period(s) before an involuntary discharge, and any short-notice exception

**There is no notice period in N.J.A.C. 10:44A.** The 2023 rewrite of 10:44A-4.2 deleted
the old 30-day planned-discharge provision that existed in the 2005 chapter
(10:44A-4.2(a)1, "at least 30 days prior to the anticipated discharge date"). What remains
is quote **NJ-5** — the provider gives "substantive evidence" to the Division, and "Until
an alternate placement is available, the individual shall continue to be served at that
residence." No notice to the resident is required by the rule at all.

The notice periods live in **tier 2, the CCP Policies & Procedures Manual v7.0.1
(September 2025)**:

- **§12.5** — all services: notify the individual, guardian and family of the intention to
  end services; give the reasons; and notify the **Support Coordinator at least 30 days
  prior** to discontinuing services. Note the 30 days runs to the *Support Coordinator*,
  not to the resident; the resident's notice has no stated minimum in §12.5.
- **§12.5.1**, residential settings, licensed and unlicensed — adds Division Circular #36,
  and: "**The individual and guardian will be given at least 60 days notice** but can move
  sooner if agreed to." Quote **NJ-6**.

**Short-notice exception and who approves it.** Division Circular #36 defines
"**Emergency**" as "the individual is in imminent peril," and "**Imminent peril**" as a
situation "which could reasonably be expected to cause serious risk to the health, safety
or welfare of the individual receiving services or another person in the current living
arrangement," adding that imminent peril "does not exist if the Division can put supports
into the living arrangement, which eliminates the serious risk." The circular is explicit
that emergency **does not include homelessness**: "It does not include homeless. If the
provider serves the individual he or she cannot become homeless in this situation."
Quote **NJ-7**.

**Who approves:** the **Division**, not the provider. Under DC#36 §VI, the provider
notifies the Regional Administrator by telephone with written follow-up; **the Division**
decides whether an emergency exists; if it does, an emergency placement is offered under
N.J.A.C. 10:46B-3.3. If there is no emergency the Regional Administrator meets with the
provider, the provider must give substantive evidence and propose modifications that would
let the placement continue, and the Division "shall consider the modifications of support
services either temporarily or long-term to allow the individual to remain in the current
living situation." Quote **NJ-8**.

## 4. Is there an appeal, and is it ADMINISTRATIVE or JUDICIAL?

**Both exist, they run in different forums, and neither is an appeal against the
provider's discharge decision as such.** Three things are called "appeal" in New Jersey
and they must be kept apart:

**(a) Administrative appeal — against the DIVISION, under N.J.A.C. 10:48.** This is the
real one. Division Circular #37, "Appeal Procedure," effective **June 24, 2026**
(rescinding the December 3, 2014 version), reproduces N.J.A.C. 10:48 in full. "Appeal"
means a review of "a disputed decision of the **Division**, which involves eligibility,
placement, or provision of service." Appeals of the denial, suspension, reduction or
termination of **waiver** services are contested and get a Medicaid **fair hearing** under
N.J.A.C. 10:49-10, referred to the **Office of Administrative Law**, with the Director of
DMAHS rendering the final decision; further appeal is to the Appellate Division of the
Superior Court. Deadline: **20 days** from the date of the notice of the Division's action
(10:48-6.2(b), 10:48-1.6(c)). For non-waiver services and eligibility the deadline is 30
days and the route is settlement conference then OAL. Quote **NJ-9**.

The object of that appeal is the Division's decision — practically, the contents of the
service plan and the placement offered. It is **not** a hearing at which the provider must
justify the discharge.

**(b) Internal provider grievance — not an appeal.** N.J.A.C. 10:44A-2.2(b)13 requires
every licensee to have "a procedure for resolving complaints and grievances of individuals
that has a minimum of two levels at which the grievance shall be heard, the last of which
involves the licensee or the executive director," and 10:44A-4.1(e)5 requires a copy to be
handed to the resident at admission. The last decision-maker is the provider's own
executive director. This is the trap the Pennsylvania pass hit: it is labelled "appealing
licensee decisions," but it never leaves the provider.

**(c) Judicial — the Special Civil Part.** If the Anti-Eviction Act applies (see Q2), the
provider must get a judgment for possession and a warrant for removal from the Superior
Court, Special Civil Part, and the resident defends there. That is a court defence, not an
administrative hearing, and DHS's STP response declines to confirm that this is the route
in DDD provider-managed settings.

**And note what the CCP manual's own "Appeals" section is not.** CCP manual §16 is
"Provider Disenrollment," and §16.2.1.3.1 "Appeals Process" belongs to the **provider**
appealing its own disenrollment from the DDD network. It has nothing to do with a
resident's discharge. Same trap, different state.

## 5. Must the NOTICE ITSELF state the appeal right?

**No — could not find any such requirement, and this is a genuine absence.**

What New Jersey has instead is weaker and sits in three places:

- **N.J.A.C. 10:48-1.3(a)** (via DC#37): "Division staff are responsible for informing
  persons served and their families/guardians about their right to appeal and to supply
  them with copies of the appeal procedure." That is a **standing duty on Division staff**,
  not a required content element of a discharge notice, and it binds the Division rather
  than the provider. Quote **NJ-10**.
- **Division Circular #36 §VI.E**: after the IHP meeting, "The individual shall be advised
  by the **Division**, in writing, of the outcome of the IHP meeting and of his or her
  right to appeal the services indicated in the IHP within 30 days." This is the closest
  New Jersey gets to the Minnesota rule — but the writing that must carry the appeal right
  is the **Division's** post-meeting outcome letter, not the **provider's** notice of
  discharge, and what may be appealed is "the services indicated in the IHP." Quote
  **NJ-11**.
- **DDD Provider Guide to the HCBS Settings Rule, p. 13**: the residency agreement
  "should" contain "Information on the individual and/or guardian's right to appeal." Not
  a rule, not mandatory language, and it is a term of the *agreement* rather than of the
  *notice*. Quote **NJ-2**.

No provision was found anywhere in N.J.A.C. 10:44A, N.J.A.C. 10:48, DC#36, DC#37, or the
CCP or Supports Program manuals requiring a provider's discharge or eviction notice to
state the appeal right. Compare Minnesota, where Minn. Stat. § 245D.10 subd. 3a(d) puts
that duty on the notice itself.

## 6. Is there a stay / "services continue pending appeal" mechanism?

**Yes for continuation of services — and it is unusually strong, because it does not even
require an appeal. The stay tied to the appeal itself is weaker and discretionary.**

**Continuation without any appeal (tier 1).** N.J.A.C. 10:44A-4.2(b)1: "Until an alternate
placement is available, the individual shall continue to be served at that residence."
Unconditional on its face — no filing, no request, no deadline. Quote **NJ-5**. The CCP
manual echoes it operationally in §12.5: the provider "will continue to support the
individual until they find services to replace those that will be discontinued and find a
new service provider(s)"; and in §12.5.1: "If the request is approved, the service provider
will continue to support the individual until they find a new service provider and place to
live and can coordinate the move." Quote **NJ-6**.

**Stay tied to the appeal (tier 1, discretionary, and narrower than it looks).**
N.J.A.C. 10:48-1.7, "Appeal of offer of placement," lets an appellant ask the **Assistant
Commissioner** to defer a placement pending the appeal, but only "**Except in
emergencies**," only if the appeal is received **10 days before the proposed placement**,
and only on one of three showings: risk of abuse or neglect, new information, or that the
placement would significantly compromise the ability to get the decision reversed. And
then: "If the Assistant Commissioner agrees to defer the placement, the Division shall not
be required to maintain the status quo **unless the Division was funding placement prior to
the request to defer**." Quote **NJ-12**.

That last clause reads in the resident's favour in the ordinary DD case — a person already
living in a DDD-funded group home *was* Division-funded before the request, so the status
quo would be maintained. But note the shape of the section: it stays an **offer of a new
placement**, not the provider's decision to stop serving. And it is discretionary, not of
right.

**Warning — the 2026 renumbering.** Division Circular #36 (2005) still cites the *old*
N.J.A.C. 10:48-1.1(e), (j), (k), (k)2 and (l) for the appeal right, the deferral, the
"maintain the status quo" duty, and the 30-day post-emergency filing window. Those
subsections **no longer exist**: N.J.A.C. 10:48 was replaced and the new DC#37 took effect
June 24, 2026. The nearest surviving equivalents are 10:48-1.3(a), 10:48-1.6(d) and
10:48-1.7. The old flat "shall maintain the status quo" is gone, replaced by the
conditional clause above, and the old 30-day window to appeal an emergency placement after
the fact has **no successor that could be located** in the new chapter. Anyone relying on
DC#36's citations should check them; DC#36 also still cites 10:44A-4.2(a)1.ii and (a)1.iii,
which the 2023 rewrite of that section deleted.

**Medicaid aid-continuing.** For waiver-service terminations the ordinary Medicaid
continuation-of-benefits rule would be the other route (request within 10 days of the
notice to keep benefits during the fair hearing). **Could not confirm at primary-source
level** — the operative subsection was not located in the fetched text of N.J.A.C.
10:49-10.3, and the 10-day figure appeared only in search summaries and advocacy handouts.
Do not publish the 10-day number on this research.

## Unresolved conflicts

1. **The central one — does the Anti-Eviction Act apply?** The statute's text contains no
   exclusion for licensed DD community residences (NJ-3), and N.J.A.C. 10:44A-4.1(d)
   assumes landlord/tenant law by requiring a lease "comparable to leases for all other
   persons in the State." But DHS's response to a public comment in its own CMS transition
   plan (NJ-4) answers the question by invoking the federal clause for "settings in which
   landlord tenant laws **do not** apply." The two cannot both be the whole truth. The most
   defensible reading of the record: the Act is not carved out, DHS does not affirmatively
   rely on it in provider-managed settings, and a resident's actual protection in practice
   comes from the residency agreement plus DC#36.

2. **Division Circular #36 cites regulations that no longer exist.** DC#36 (2005) is still
   the operative discharge policy — the CCP manual v7.0.1 (Sept 2025) points at it by name
   twice, and the 2023 transition plan cites it to CMS. But its "POLICIES" section cites
   N.J.A.C. 10:44A-4.2(a)1.ii and (a)1.iii, deleted in the 2023 rewrite of that section, and
   N.J.A.C. 10:48-1.1(e), (j), (k), (k)2 and (l), all superseded by the chapter reproduced
   in DC#37 effective June 24, 2026. Most consequentially, the old 10:48-1.1(k)2 "requires
   the Division to maintain the status quo if the Division Director agrees to defer a
   transfer or discharge"; the successor, 10:48-1.7(a)2, makes that conditional on the
   Division having been funding the placement beforehand. DC#36 also assigns the deferral
   decision to the "Division Director" while 10:48-1.7 assigns it to the "Assistant
   Commissioner."

3. **Whose notice, and how long.** N.J.A.C. 10:44A imposes **no** notice period on a
   provider. The CCP manual imposes 30 days on notice to the Support Coordinator and 60 days
   on notice to the individual and guardian, but the manual is tier 2 — a policy document,
   not a rule — and the two figures are stated in different bullet lists in different
   subsections. A provider following only the licensing chapter would violate neither.

4. **"Appeal" in §12.5.1 vs. what 10:48 actually gives.** CCP §12.5.1 says "The individual
   and guardian have the right to appeal based on the policies outlined in DC#36." DC#36's
   appeal is an appeal of "the services indicated in the IHP" against the **Division**, and
   under the current N.J.A.C. 10:48 the object of an appeal is "a disputed decision of the
   Division." A provider's decision to discharge is not itself the appealable act. This is
   the same ambiguity the pilot flagged: the reader is told they can appeal, without being
   told they are appealing something other than the discharge.
