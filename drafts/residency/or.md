# Oregon — residency agreement and involuntary discharge

- **State:** Oregon (OR)
- **Researched:** 2026-07-30
- **Researcher:** agent (Claude research pass, per `drafts/residency-research-scope.md`)
- **Primary facility category answered:** ODDS **24-hour residential programs and settings** for
  children and adults with intellectual or developmental disabilities — OAR chapter 411, division 325.
  These are the licensed DD group homes (five or fewer = "residential training home", six or more =
  "residential training facility" under ORS 443.400).
- **Second category checked and reported separately:** ODDS **adult foster homes for individuals with
  I/DD (AFH-DD)** — OAR chapter 411, division 360. Its discharge rules are nearly identical but not
  word-for-word the same, and it has an extra immediate-exit provision the group-home rule lacks.
- **Not answered here:** ICF/IID, APD assisted living / residential care (OAR 411-054), OHA
  residential treatment (OAR 309-035), and ODDS supported living (OAR 411-328). Different categories,
  different rules. Do not import these answers into those.

Oregon is a **strong** state on this topic. It answers yes to all seven questions, including the two
the scope document expects most states to fail (notice must state the appeal right; a stay exists).
On the two hardest asks Oregon is at least as protective as Minnesota, and its residency-agreement
content requirement is stronger than California's.

---

## 1. Is a written residency/admission agreement required? Which regulation?

**Yes. Two independent requirements.**

- **OAR 411-004-0020(2)(c)** — Oregon's HCBS settings rule, applying to every provider-owned,
  controlled, or operated residential setting: the unit "may be owned, rented, or occupied by an
  individual under a legally enforceable Residency Agreement."
- **OAR 411-325-0300(1)(a)** — the DD 24-hour residential rule: "A provider must enter into a written
  Residency Agreement with each individual" specifying, at minimum, (A) the rights and
  responsibilities of the individual and the provider, and **(B) "The eviction process, notice
  requirements, and appeal rights available to each individual."**

Subsection (1)(e) additionally requires the provider to review and hand over a copy at entry and at
least **90 calendar days before any change**, documented by the individual's signature, with the
signed copy kept in the record.

For AFH-DD the parallel requirement is **OAR 411-360-0055** (Provider Enrollment Agreements,
Contracts, and Residency Agreements).

Note the contrast with the California pilot: California's `22 CCR § 80068(c)(7)` requires only that
the agreement list "the conditions under which your agreement can be ended." Oregon requires the
agreement to state the **eviction process, the notice requirements, AND the appeal rights**. This is
the single most quotable sentence in the Oregon pack.

## 2. Does state landlord/tenant law apply, or is there a carve-out?

**There is an express statutory carve-out. The Oregon Residential Landlord and Tenant Act does NOT
apply.** This is the `(vi)(A)` hinge, and Oregon lands on the second branch of it.

**ORS 90.113** ("Additional exclusion from application of chapter"): "Residence in a licensed program,
facility or home described in ORS 430.306 to 430.375, 430.380, 430.381, 430.397 to 430.401, 430.405 to
430.565, 430.570, 430.590, 430.709, **443.400 to 443.455, 443.705 to 443.825** or 443.835 is not
governed by this chapter."

- ORS 443.400 to 443.455 is the licensing scheme for residential facilities and homes, and ORS 443.400
  defines "residential training facility" (six or more individuals with I/DD) and "residential
  training home" (five or fewer). These are the DD group homes governed by OAR 411-325. OAR
  411-325-0390's own "Statutes Implemented" line cites ORS 443.400-443.445, 443.450, 443.455.
- ORS 443.705 to 443.825 is the adult foster home scheme, covering AFH-DD.

So both DD group homes and DD adult foster homes are named out of ORS chapter 90 by cross-reference.
(ORS 90.110(1) separately excludes institutional residence incidental to the provision of medical or
similar service; 90.113 is the cleaner and more specific cite for DD settings and is the one to use.)

Because landlord/tenant law does not apply, **OAR 411-004-0020(2)(c)** second sentence governs: "For a
setting in which landlord tenant laws do not apply, the Residency Agreement must provide protections
for the individual and address eviction and appeal processes. The eviction and appeal processes must
be substantially equivalent to the processes provided under landlord tenant laws."

Oregon's own Statewide Transition Plan says the same thing in plainer words and confirms the state
knows it is on the second branch: "Most provider-owned or controlled settings in the DD system are
exempt per statute from landlord tenant laws. DD settings rules reflect notice of exit requirements
that are comparable to landlord tenant law eviction processes and protections." (STP, 02-22-2019,
p. 185.) Per the source hierarchy this is cited as a finding aid, not as the authority — the
authority is ORS 90.113 plus OAR 411-004-0020(2)(c).

## 3. Notice period(s) before involuntary discharge, short-notice exception, and who approves it

**Category: DD 24-hour residential (OAR 411-325-0390(5)).**

- **Standard notice: 30 calendar days**, advance and written. "A provider must not reduce services,
  transfer, or exit an individual involuntarily without giving advance written notice 30 calendar days
  prior to the reduction, transfer, or exit." (5)(b)
- Involuntary action is allowed only for the seven enumerated reasons in (5)(a): imminent risk of
  danger to self or others; a medical emergency creating support needs the provider cannot meet;
  service needs exceeding the provider's ability; nonpayment for services or room and board; license
  suspended/revoked/not renewed/surrendered; Medicaid enrollment or contract terminated; or
  certification/endorsement suspended/revoked/not renewed/surrendered.
- **Notice must go to four parties**: the adult individual, the legal or designated representative,
  **the Oregon Residential Facility Ombudsman's office**, and the case manager. (5)(b)(A)
- **Short-notice exception:** less than 30 days is permitted "only in a medical emergency or when an
  individual is engaging in behavior that poses an imminent danger to self or others in the home and
  undue delay in moving the individual increases the risk of harm." (5)(c)
- **Who approves the short notice: no one, in advance.** The rule says notice must be given "on the
  applicable Department form ... immediately upon the provider's determination of the need." This is
  a provider determination with immediate notification to the Ombudsman and case manager, **not** a
  prior-approval scheme. This differs from California, where a 3-day notice requires advance Community
  Care Licensing approval. Oregon's counterweight is on the back end instead: under (6)(c) a
  short-notice exit that is appealed forces the provider to hold the room and keep serving the person
  until the Final Order.
- Before acting, the provider "must demonstrate through documentation, attempts to resolve the reason
  for the involuntary reduction, transfer, or exit, including consideration of alternatives ... and
  engagement of the case manager." (5)(d)
- Separately, **voluntary closure of the home also requires 30 calendar days' written notice**
  (411-325-0390(8)(a)), including transfers between two homes owned by the same provider ((8)(b)).

**Category: AFH-DD (OAR 411-360-0190(5)).** Same 30-calendar-day rule and the same medical-emergency /
imminent-danger short-notice exception. Two differences worth recording: the notice list is the
individual, representative, and case manager (the Ombudsman's office is not named in 411-360-0190(5)(b)
the way it is in 411-325-0390(5)(b)(A)); and 411-360-0190(9) adds an **immediate exit with no advance
notice** for a narrow sex-offense-supervision-plus-current-risk situation, which **does** require the
provider to phone ODDS Central Office and get a Department review before the move.

## 4. Is there an appeal, and is it ADMINISTRATIVE or JUDICIAL?

**Administrative.** A contested case hearing under **ORS chapter 183** (Oregon's Administrative
Procedures Act), before the **Office of Administrative Hearings**, ending in a Final Order.

OAR 411-325-0390(6)(a): "An individual must be given the opportunity for a hearing under ORS chapter
183 and OAR 411-318-0030 to dispute an involuntary reduction, transfer, or exit, except when a
provider's license is revoked, not renewed, voluntarily surrendered, or the provider's Medicaid
contract is terminated."

Mechanics, from **OAR 411-318-0030** (Contested Case Hearings for Provider Notices of Involuntary
Reductions, Transfers, or Exits):

- The request may be made **orally or in writing** (subsection (3)) — orally to a CDDP, Brokerage, or
  Department staff person, who must then file the form within one business day. This is a materially
  lower bar than filing an answer in an eviction case.
- ODDS refers the request to the Office of Administrative Hearings within five business days, assists
  the claimant in gathering and submitting exhibits, and acts as liaison. (subsection (4))
- An **expedited hearing** is available under (8) in two situations: when the hearing was requested
  more than 15 days after the notice and the person wants services continued, or when the person got
  less than 30 days' notice and has not been allowed to remain in the setting.
- The rule does **not** apply to providers in a direct employer-employee relationship with the
  individual or the individual's employer representative. (subsection (1))

Naming the three things the scope document says to keep distinct: this is **not** a court defense to
an eviction (ORS chapter 90 does not apply at all here, per ORS 90.113), and it is **not** merely an
internal provider grievance. Oregon does also have an internal/agency complaint track under OAR
411-318-0015, which is a separate and weaker thing; do not conflate them.

The one gap: the hearing right is expressly **unavailable** when the provider's license is revoked,
not renewed, or surrendered, or its Medicaid contract is terminated. In those cases the person still
moves, and the only protection is the closure-notice rule.

## 5. Must the NOTICE ITSELF state the appeal right?

**Yes — this is the strongest finding, and Oregon passes.**

OAR 411-325-0390(5)(b)(B): "The written notice must be provided on the applicable Department form and
include: (i) The reason for the reduction, transfer, or exit; and **(ii) The right of the individual to
a hearing as described in section (6) of this rule.**"

It is belt-and-braces: it is not only a content requirement, it is a **prescribed state form** — the
Notice of Involuntary Reduction, Transfer, or Exit, ODDS forms **0719R / 0719C / 0719E**, named in
OAR 411-318-0030(3) — and the Administrative Hearing Request is **included with** that notice form, so
the request mechanism physically travels with the notice.

AFH-DD carries the identical requirement at OAR 411-360-0190(5)(b)(A)(ii).

Separately, OAR 411-325-0300(1)(a)(B) requires appeal rights to be in the Residency Agreement itself,
and 411-325-0300(5)(c) requires a notice of hearing rights to be explained and provided at entry, on
request, and annually.

## 6. Is there a stay / "services continue pending appeal"?

**Yes, three overlapping mechanisms.**

1. **Automatic continuation on a timely request.** OAR 411-325-0390(6)(b): "If an individual requests
   a hearing within 15 calendar days after the date of the notice and requests continuation of
   services, the individual must receive the same services until the hearing is resolved." Same rule
   at OAR 411-318-0030(6) ("until receipt of a Final Order") and at OAR 411-360-0190(6)(b) for AFH-DD.
   Note it is not fully automatic — the person must both request the hearing within 15 days **and**
   request continuation.
2. **Room reservation after a short-notice exit.** OAR 411-325-0390(6)(c): where less than 30 days'
   notice was given under (5)(c) and the individual has requested a hearing, "the provider must reserve
   the room of the individual and deliver services according to the individual's needs until receipt
   of the Final Order." OAR 411-318-0030(7) says the same. This is the provision that makes the
   emergency exception survivable.
3. **Baseline duty to serve.** OAR 411-325-0390(5)(e): "A provider is responsible for the provision of
   services until the date of reduction, transfer, or exit identified in the notice, or when an
   individual requests a hearing, until the hearing is resolved."

Where a person misses the 15-day window and still wants services continued, the route is the
**expedited hearing** under OAR 411-318-0030(8)(a).
